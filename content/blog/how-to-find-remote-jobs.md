To find remote jobs, search job boards with the "remote" filter and also with terms like "work from home", "distributed", and "anywhere in the US". Then check the location rules in each post, because many remote jobs are open only in certain states or time zones. Target companies that already work remotely, use your network for referrals, and tailor each application to show that you work well without an office. Avoid any "remote job" that asks you to pay money or share bank details before an offer.

## Know the Types of Remote Jobs

The word "remote" means different things in different posts. Read the details before you apply.

| Type | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Fully remote | You can work from anywhere, often within one country |
| Remote within a region | You must live in certain states or time zones |
| Remote first | Most of the team is remote, but there may be an office |
| Hybrid | You work from an office on some days each week |
| Temporary remote | The role is remote now but may move to an office later |
| Remote with travel | You work from home but travel for meetings or clients |

Decide which types you accept before you start. This saves you from long interview processes for jobs that do not fit.

## How to Find Remote Jobs: 7 Search Methods

### 1. Use Filters on Large Job Boards

Most large job boards have a remote filter. Use it, but do not trust it alone. Some posts that are marked remote are hybrid, and some fully remote jobs are not marked. Read each post.

### 2. Search With More Than One Term

Companies describe remote work in different ways. Search with each of these terms plus your job title:

- "remote"
- "work from home"
- "distributed team"
- "anywhere in the US"
- "virtual"
- "telecommute"

### 3. Use Remote-Focused Job Boards

Some job boards list only remote jobs. They can save you time, because you do not need to filter out office roles. Check that a board shows the company name and links to the company's own careers page.

### 4. Target Remote-First Companies

Companies that already run fully remote teams hire remotely all year. Make a list of 20 to 30 such companies in your field. Many of them say on their careers page that they are remote first or distributed. Check their careers pages each week.

### 5. Use Your Network

Ask former coworkers who work remotely how their company hires. Ask about open roles and referrals. A referral can be especially helpful for remote roles, because they may attract many applicants. This guide on [how to ask for a referral](/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-referral) has message templates.

### 6. Set Job Alerts

Create saved searches with your title, the word "remote", and your country or state. Turn on daily email alerts. Early applications are easier for a recruiter to see before the list gets long.

### 7. Look at Contract and Freelance Work

Some remote roles start as contracts. A contract can turn into a full-time role, and it gives you recent remote experience for your resume. Check the terms carefully, because contract work often has different taxes and benefits.

## Check the Location Rules in Every Post

This is the step that most applicants skip. A remote job in the US is often limited to certain states. The employer must follow payroll, tax, and employment rules in each state where staff live.

Look for lines like these in the post:

- "Remote, US only"
- "Remote in [list of states]"
- "Must work [time zone] hours"
- "Must live within [distance] of [city]"

If you live outside the listed area, do not apply unless the post says that the list may change. Rules differ by country too, so a "remote" job in another country usually does not mean that you can work from anywhere in the world.

## Make Your Application Remote-Ready

Remote employers often look for proof that you can work well on your own. Show it in your materials.

### Your Resume

- Add "Remote" next to past remote roles, for example "Data Analyst, [Company] (Remote)".
- Mention remote tools that you use, such as video meeting software, chat tools, and project management tools.
- Add results that show independent work and clear communication.

Then [tailor your resume to the job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description) for each role.

### Your Cover Letter

Add one or two sentences about how you work remotely. Use a specific example.

> I have worked remotely for [number] years with a team across [number] time zones. At [Company], I [result], and I kept the project on track with written weekly updates and short video check-ins. I am comfortable working on my own and I write clearly, which matters on a team like yours at [Company].

### Your Interview

Expect questions such as "How do you stay organized when you work from home?" and "How do you communicate with a team you do not see every day?" Prepare a short story for each one. Test your camera, microphone, and internet connection before every video interview.

## How to Spot Remote Job Scams

Fake remote jobs are a real risk, because remote work is in demand and the whole process can happen online. Watch for these warning signs:

- The employer asks you to pay for training, equipment, or a starter kit.
- They send you a check and ask you to send part of the money back.
- The interview happens only by text chat or instant messaging.
- The offer comes very fast, with no real interview.
- They ask for your bank details or your Social Security number before a written offer.
- The email comes from a free email address, not the company's domain.
- The pay is much higher than normal for the work.

If you are not sure, find the company's official website on your own and contact them through it. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) also publishes advice on job scams on its consumer website.

## Keep Your Search Organized

Remote searches often involve many applications to companies in different places. Track each one with the company, the role, the location rules, the date, and the next step. If you search while you still have a job, this guide on [how to look for a job while employed](/blog/job-search-while-employed) shows how to keep the search private and steady.

## Checklist Before You Apply

- The post is fully remote, or the type of remote work fits you.
- You live in an allowed state, country, or time zone.
- The company is real, and the post links to its own careers page.
- Your resume shows remote experience or independent work.
- Your cover letter has one specific example of how you work remotely.
- Nobody asked you for money or bank details.

Learning how to find remote jobs takes a little more care than an office search. Use several search terms, check the location rules, and apply to fewer roles with stronger applications.
