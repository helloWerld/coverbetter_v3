The most useful video interview tips are simple. Test your technology the day before, and sit facing a light source. Put the camera at eye level, use headphones with a microphone, and look at the camera when you speak. Join the call five minutes early. Keep your answers a little shorter than in person, and have a backup plan if your internet fails.

A video interview is still an interview. The questions and the hiring decision are the same as in person. But the camera changes how you come across, and small technical problems can break your focus. Prepare for the setup as carefully as you prepare for the questions.

## Before the Day: Test Everything

Do a full test at least one day before the interview, at the same time of day and in the same room.

- Install the meeting app the employer uses (for example, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, or Google Meet) and update it.
- Open the invitation link and confirm that it works.
- Check your display name. Use your real first and last name.
- Test your camera, microphone, and speakers inside the app, not only in your system settings.
- Record a short test call and watch it. Look at your light, your background, and your sound.
- Check your internet speed. If your connection is weak, use a cable or sit near your router.
- Charge your laptop and keep the charger plugged in during the call.

If the employer uses a platform you do not know, ask the recruiter for a test link or any instructions.

## Set Up Your Camera, Light, and Sound

### Camera

Put the camera at eye level. Stack books under your laptop if you need to. Sit so that the frame shows your head and the top of your shoulders, with a little space above your head. A camera that looks up at you from a desk is the most common problem, and it is easy to fix.

### Light

Face a window or a lamp. Light from in front of you makes your face clear. Light from behind you (for example, a window behind your chair) makes your face dark. If the light is uneven, put a lamp behind your laptop and point it at a wall in front of you.

### Sound

Sound matters more than video. An interviewer can work with a slightly blurry picture, but not with an echo or noise. Use wired headphones with a microphone or a headset if you can. Choose a room with soft surfaces, such as a bedroom, because they reduce echo. Close windows and tell other people in your home when the call starts.

### Background

Choose a plain, tidy background. A wall, a bookshelf, or a neat room is fine. Remove anything personal or distracting. A blur effect is usually acceptable, but some virtual backgrounds make your edges flicker. Test it first.

## Video Interview Tips for Body Language

On camera, the interviewer sees only your face and shoulders. That makes small habits more visible.

- Look at the camera when you speak, not at the interviewer's face on screen. This looks like eye contact to them. Put a small sticker near the camera as a reminder.
- Look at the screen when you listen, so you can see their reactions.
- Sit up straight and lean forward a little. It shows interest.
- Nod and smile while you listen. On video, the interviewer cannot hear small sounds like "mm-hm" if your microphone suppresses noise.
- Keep your hands in the frame sometimes when you explain something. Natural gestures help.
- Do not look at a second screen or your phone during the call.

## How to Answer Questions on Video

Video calls have a small delay. People often talk over each other because of it. Use these habits to keep the conversation smooth:

- Pause for one second after the interviewer stops before you answer.
- Keep answers shorter than in person. Aim for one to two minutes for most answers.
- Structure each answer so that it is easy to follow. For behavioral questions, the [STAR method](/blog/star-method-interview) works well.
- End each answer clearly, so the interviewer knows you have finished. For example: "That is how I handled it."
- If you did not hear the full question, ask: "Sorry, the sound cut out. Could you repeat the last part?"

Prepare answers to the questions that come up in most interviews, especially "[tell me about yourself](/blog/tell-me-about-yourself)." A clear first answer helps you relax for the rest of the call.

## What to Do When Something Goes Wrong

Technical problems happen. Interviewers know this. How you handle the problem matters more than the problem.

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Your video freezes | Turn off your video for a moment and keep talking. Say what happened. |
| The sound cuts out | Say, "I think I lost you for a moment. Could you repeat that?" |
| The call drops | Rejoin the same link at once. If it fails, email the interviewer and offer to continue by phone. |
| A person or pet comes into the room | Apologize briefly, mute if needed, and continue. Do not over-explain. |
| Loud noise outside | Mute while the interviewer speaks. Mention it once if it continues. |

Before the call, write down the interviewer's email and phone number, or the recruiter's. Then you can contact them at once if the call fails.

## What to Wear

Dress as you would for an in-person interview at the same company, at least from the waist up. Wear the full outfit, so you are not surprised if you have to stand. Choose solid colors. Thin stripes and small patterns can flicker on camera. Avoid colors that match your background.

## Day-Of Checklist

- Restart your computer one hour before the call.
- Close all other apps and browser tabs. Turn off notifications.
- Put your phone on silent and out of reach.
- Have water, a pen, and paper on the desk.
- Put the job post, your resume, and your questions for the interviewer in one window you can see quickly.
- Check your light and camera position one last time.
- Join the call five minutes early.
- Smile when the interviewer joins and say their name.

## One-Way Recorded Interviews

Some employers use one-way video interviews. You record answers to written questions, and someone reviews the videos later. The same setup tips apply. In addition:

- Read the instructions about time limits and retakes before you start.
- Do a practice question if the platform offers one.
- Speak to the camera as if a person is there.
- Use the full answer time only if you need it. A clear, short answer is fine.

## After the Video Interview

Write down the questions you were asked and your notes about the role while they are fresh. Then send a short thank-you email within 24 hours. Our guide to the [thank-you email after an interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview) has templates.

Some employers have their own rules for video interviews, such as a required platform or camera on for the whole call. Always follow the instructions in the invitation.
