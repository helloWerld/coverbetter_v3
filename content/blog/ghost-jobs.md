Ghost jobs are job postings for roles that the company does not plan to fill soon, or at all. A company may leave an old posting online, collect resumes for later, or post to look like it is growing. To spot ghost jobs, check how old the posting is and whether it appears on the company's own careers page. Also check if the company is hiring for that team, and if a real person replies when you ask.

Ghost jobs waste your most limited resource: time. A tailored application can take an hour or more. When you send that work to a job that is not real, you get silence and start to doubt yourself. The problem is usually the posting, not you.

## Why Companies Post Ghost Jobs

Most ghost jobs do not come from bad intent. Common reasons include:

- The company filled the role, but nobody removed the posting.
- The company froze the hiring budget after it posted the job.
- The company wants a pool of resumes for future openings.
- An internal candidate already has the job, but the policy requires an external posting.
- A recruiting agency posts a general role to collect candidates for several clients.
- The company wants to look like it is growing, to customers, investors, or its own employees.
- A manager wants to see who is available before deciding to hire.

From the outside, you often cannot tell which reason applies. That is why you check the signs and limit how much time you invest.

## Warning Signs of Ghost Jobs

No single sign proves that a posting is a ghost job. Look for several signs together.

| Sign | Why it matters | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The posting is many weeks or months old | Old postings are more likely to be filled or frozen. | Look at the posted date. Note that some sites refresh dates when a job is reposted. |
| The same job is reposted again and again | The company may collect resumes without a plan to hire. | Search the exact title and company. Check if it reappears every few weeks. |
| It is not on the company's careers page | The posting may be old or copied by another site. | Search the company's own careers page. |
| The description is vague | Real roles usually have clear tasks and a team. | Look for a team name, a manager title, and specific duties. |
| No salary range or location detail | Real, budgeted roles often have more details, though this varies. | Compare with the company's other postings. |
| The company had recent layoffs or a hiring freeze | New hiring may be on hold. | Search recent news about the company. |
| Many applicants and no replies | Signs of a role that nobody is reviewing. | Ask people who work there, or check if anyone got an interview. |
| "Always accepting applications" | This often means a talent pool, not a current opening. | Read the posting text closely. |

Some job sites show the date that a posting first appeared, while others show the last update. Treat dates as a clue, not as proof.

## How to Check a Job Before You Apply

Use these steps for any role where you plan to write a custom application. They take 10 to 15 minutes.

1. Find the posting on the company's official careers page. If it is not there, be careful.
2. Check the dates. Search for the title to see if older copies of the same posting exist.
3. Look at the team on LinkedIn. Find people with related titles and check if the team is growing or shrinking.
4. Read recent news about the company, such as layoffs, funding, or a new office.
5. Find the recruiter or hiring manager. If you can, send a short message to ask if the role is active.

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I am interested in the [Job Title] role posted on [date or site]. Before I apply, can you confirm that the role is still open and actively hiring? I have [one-line summary of your fit], and I want to make sure my application reaches the right team.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

If the recruiter replies and confirms the role, you now have a contact. If nobody replies, you have learned something too.

## How to Protect Your Time

You cannot avoid every ghost job. But you can reduce how much time you lose.

### Use two levels of effort

- Full effort: a tailored resume and cover letter. Use this for roles that pass your checks and fit you well.
- Quick effort: a short application with your standard resume. Use this for good matches where you have doubts about the posting.

### Apply early

Fresh postings are more likely to be real and active. Set job alerts so you can apply within the first few days. This also helps with real jobs, because some employers review applications as they arrive.

### Do not rely only on postings

Many real roles are filled through contacts, sometimes before a posting goes up. Spend part of each week on networking. Our guide to the [hidden job market](/blog/hidden-job-market) explains how.

### Track everything

Keep a record of where and when you applied, and whether anyone replied. After a few weeks, you will see patterns: companies that never reply and sites that send you to old postings.

## Ghost Jobs Compared With Job Scams

Ghost jobs and job scams are different problems, but some signs overlap.

| | Ghost job | Job scam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who posts it | Usually a real company | A scammer, often pretending to be a real company |
| What you lose | Time and energy | Money, personal data, or both |
| Typical signs | Old posting, no reply, vague role | Requests for payment, fast offers, chat-only contact |
| What to do | Limit your effort and move on | Stop contact and report it |

If a posting asks you for money or for your bank details early, treat it as a possible scam, not a ghost job. Read our guide to [fake job postings and job scams](/blog/job-scams) for the full checklist.

## What to Do If You Think You Applied to a Ghost Job

- Follow up once, about one or two weeks after you apply. A short message to the recruiter is enough. See our guide on [how to follow up on a job application](/blog/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application).
- If there is no reply, mark the application as closed in your tracker and move on.
- Keep the tailored material. You can reuse much of it for similar roles.
- Do not take the silence personally. When a posting is not real, no application could succeed.

## Keep Your Effort Where It Pays Off

Ghost jobs are frustrating, but they are a known part of the job search now. The best answer is a simple system. Check before you invest, apply early, and use two levels of effort. Build contacts who can tell you about real openings.

Rules about job posting disclosures differ by state, province, and country, and some places are changing them. Check local rules if you want to know what employers must disclose where you live.
