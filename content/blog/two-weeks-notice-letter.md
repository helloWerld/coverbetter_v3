A two weeks notice letter is a short, formal letter that tells your employer you are resigning. It states your last working day, usually two weeks from the date you give it. Include the date, your manager's name, a clear sentence that you resign, and your exact last day. Add a brief thank-you and an offer to help with the handover. Keep it to one short page, and do not explain your reasons in detail.

The letter is a record for HR, not a conversation. The conversation happens first, with your manager, in person or on a video call. The letter then confirms what you said, in writing.

## What to Include in a Two Weeks Notice Letter

| Part | What to write | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date | The date you give the letter | Yes |
| Greeting | Your manager's name | Yes |
| Resignation sentence | "I am resigning from my position as [Job Title]." | Yes |
| Last working day | The exact date | Yes |
| Thank-you | One or two sentences | Recommended |
| Handover offer | How you will help during the transition | Recommended |
| Contact details | Personal email for after you leave | Optional |
| Signature | Your full name | Yes |

Leave out complaints, details about your new job, and long explanations. HR may keep this letter in your file for years.

## Steps to Resign Well

1. Sign your new offer first. Do not resign until you have a written offer and any conditions (such as a background check) are complete.
2. Check your contract and the employee handbook. Look for the required notice period, rules about unused vacation, and any agreements about bonuses, equipment, or confidentiality.
3. Tell your manager first, in a private conversation. Your manager should not hear it from a coworker or from HR.
4. Give the letter right after the conversation, by email or on paper, and copy HR if that is your company's practice.
5. Make a handover plan. List your open tasks, deadlines, contacts, and files.
6. Work fully until your last day. People remember how you leave.
7. Return company equipment, and ask HR about your final paycheck and benefits.

Rules on final pay, unused vacation, and benefits differ by state and by employer. Check your company policy and your state's labor department website for the rules that apply to you.

## Two Weeks Notice Letter Templates

Replace every item in square brackets. For the email version, use a subject line like "Resignation: [Your Name]."

### Template 1: Standard two weeks notice letter

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Manager Name],
>
> Please accept this letter as formal notice that I am resigning from my position as [Job Title] at [Company]. My last working day will be [Date].
>
> Thank you for the opportunities I had during my time here. I learned a lot from you and the team, especially about [specific skill or area].
>
> During the next two weeks, I will do my best to finish my current work and hand over my tasks. Please let me know how I can help make the transition smooth.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

### Template 2: Short and simple

Use this when you want to keep the letter neutral.

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Manager Name],
>
> I am writing to give notice that I am resigning from my position as [Job Title]. My last day will be [Date].
>
> I will help with the handover of my work before I leave.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

### Template 3: Warm, for a good relationship

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Manager Name],
>
> This letter confirms my resignation from my position as [Job Title] at [Company]. My last working day will be [Date].
>
> This was not an easy decision. I am grateful for your support over the past [number] years, and for the chance to work on [project or achievement]. I will miss working with this team.
>
> Before I leave, I will document my open projects, train [Name or "my replacement"] on [key tasks], and make sure all deadlines are covered. I would like to stay in touch, and you can reach me at [personal email].
>
> With thanks,
> [Your Name]

### Template 4: Notice period longer than two weeks

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Manager Name],
>
> Please accept this letter as my formal resignation from my position as [Job Title]. As required by my contract, I am giving [number] weeks of notice, and my last working day will be [Date].
>
> Thank you for the support and the opportunities during my time at [Company]. I will work with you to plan a complete handover of my responsibilities before I leave.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

## How to Have the Resignation Conversation

Plan what you will say before you meet your manager. Keep it short and calm.

A simple script:

"Thank you for meeting with me. I want to tell you that I have accepted a position at another company, and I am resigning. My last day will be [Date]. I have really valued working with you, and I want to make the handover as easy as possible for the team. I will send you my letter today."

Your manager may ask questions. Prepare for these:

- "Where are you going?" You can share it or say, "I prefer to keep that private for now."
- "Why are you leaving?" Give one short, positive reason, such as "I want to grow in [area]."
- "Is there anything that would make you stay?"

## How to Handle a Counteroffer

Some employers make a counteroffer: more pay, a new title, or other changes. Think before you answer. Ask yourself why you started to look for a new job. If the reasons were not only about money, a raise may not fix them.

If you want to decline the counteroffer, say: "Thank you, I appreciate the offer. I have made my decision, and I will keep my last day as [Date]." If you are unsure, our method for [how to compare job offers](/blog/how-to-compare-job-offers) can help you compare the counteroffer with the new job.

## Common Mistakes

- You resign before you have a signed offer.
- You send the letter before you talk to your manager.
- You forget to state an exact last day.
- You write complaints or criticism in the letter.
- You tell coworkers before your manager.
- You stop working hard during the notice period.
- You delete files or take company information with you. Leave everything that belongs to the company.

If you are still searching and not ready to resign, read our guide on how to manage a [job search while employed](/blog/job-search-while-employed).

## What Happens After You Give Notice

Your employer may ask you to work the full notice period, a shorter period, or none of it. Some companies end access to systems on the same day. Prepare for that: before your meeting, save any personal files and personal contacts from your work devices, as long as company policy allows it. Do not copy company data.

Ask HR about your final paycheck, unused paid time off, retirement accounts, and health insurance continuation. In the US, many employers that offer group health insurance must offer COBRA continuation coverage. The rules depend on the employer and your situation, so ask HR for the details.

Notice customs and legal rules differ outside the US. In many countries, contracts or national law set the notice period, and it can be one month or longer.
