A resume should be one page if you have less than about 10 years of relevant experience. Use two pages if you have more experience that relates to the job. Most US resumes should not be longer than two pages. The real rule is relevance: every line must help the reader decide that you fit this job. When you are unsure, choose the shorter version and keep only your strongest, most recent proof.

Resume length causes a lot of worry, but the answer is simpler than it looks. A hiring manager wants to find your fit quickly. Length only matters because extra lines make that harder.

## How Long Should a Resume Be at Each Career Stage?

Use this table as a starting point. Then adjust for your field and the job.

| Career stage | Usual length | What to focus on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student or new graduate | 1 page | Education, internships, projects, part-time jobs, skills |
| 1 to 5 years of experience | 1 page | Recent roles, results, and skills from the job post |
| 5 to 10 years of experience | 1 page, sometimes 2 | Last 2 or 3 roles in detail, older roles short |
| 10 or more years of experience | 2 pages | Last 10 to 15 years in detail, leadership, and scope |
| Senior executive | 2 pages, sometimes more | Results at company level, board or leadership work |
| Academic or research role | As long as needed (a CV) | Publications, grants, teaching, conferences |

These are norms, not laws. A designer with a strong portfolio link might keep one page at any stage. A software engineer with 12 years and many relevant projects might need two.

## When One Page Is the Right Choice

Choose one page in these cases:

- You have less than about 10 years of work experience.
- You are changing careers, and most of your past work is in another field.
- You apply for an entry-level or junior role.
- Your older jobs are similar, and they repeat the same skills.
- The posting or the recruiter asks for one page.

A one-page resume forces you to choose. That is good. When you choose your best five or six results, the reader sees them at once.

## When Two Pages Is the Right Choice

Choose two pages in these cases:

- You have 10 or more years of experience that relates to the job.
- You manage people, budgets, or large projects, and you need room for scope and results.
- Your field values technical detail, such as engineering, IT, or healthcare, and the details match the post.
- You have certifications, licenses, or security clearances that the job requires.

If you use two pages, fill at least half of the second page. A second page with three lines looks like a formatting mistake. Put your name and page number on the second page, in case someone prints it.

## What to Cut When Your Resume Is Too Long

If your resume is too long, cut in this order. Start with the content that helps the reader least.

1. Remove the objective statement. Replace it with a short summary only if it adds value. See our [resume summary examples](/blog/resume-summary-examples).
2. Remove "References available upon request." Employers assume this.
3. Cut jobs older than about 15 years, or list them in one line each with no bullets.
4. Cut bullet points that describe duties instead of results.
5. Merge similar bullet points into one.
6. Remove skills that everyone in your field has (for example, "Microsoft Word" for an office role).
7. Remove your full street address. A city and state are enough.
8. Cut hobbies and interests unless they relate to the job.
9. Shorten your education section if you have work experience. Remove high school, coursework, and GPA unless they help.
10. Remove bullet points that do not relate to this job post.

The last step is the most powerful. When you tailor your resume to each job, the length often solves itself. Our guide on how to [tailor your resume to a job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description) shows the method.

## How to Make Each Line Shorter

Many resumes are long because each bullet point is long. Use this pattern: action verb, what you did, and the result.

Before: "Was responsible for the management of the company's social media accounts, which included the creation of content and the scheduling of posts on a daily basis."

After: "Managed 4 company social media accounts and scheduled daily posts."

More tips:

- Start every bullet with a verb. Delete "Responsible for."
- Remove words like "successfully," "various," and "a number of."
- Use numerals for numbers (for example, "4" and not "four").
- Keep most bullets to one line, and never more than two.
- Use three to five bullets for recent roles and one or two for older roles.

## Formatting Rules That Keep the Length Honest

Do not use formatting tricks to squeeze a long resume onto one page. Tiny text and narrow margins make your resume hard to read.

- Use a body font size of 10 to 12 points.
- Keep margins of about 0.5 to 1 inch.
- Use clear headings and white space between sections.
- Use one simple column for the main content. Some applicant tracking systems read complex layouts in the wrong order, and this varies by system.
- Save as PDF unless the employer asks for Word.

If the text fits only with tiny type, the resume is too long. Cut content instead.

## Special Cases

### Employment gaps

Length does not hide a gap. Short, clear dates and a one-line explanation work better than extra detail. Read our guide on how to handle an [employment gap on a resume](/blog/employment-gap-on-resume).

### Federal, academic, and international applications

US federal job applications often have their own resume rules, and these can change. Academic jobs usually ask for a CV, which lists all publications and can be many pages. In the UK, Europe, and many other countries, "CV" means a resume, and a two-page CV is common. Always follow the instructions in the posting.

### Recruiter requests

If a recruiter asks for a specific length or format, follow the request. Recruiters know what their clients want to see.

## A Quick Length Check

Before you send your resume, answer these questions:

- Does every bullet relate to this job post?
- Is your most relevant experience on the top half of page one?
- Did you cut jobs older than about 15 years or shorten them to one line?
- Is the font 10 points or larger?
- If you have two pages, is the second page at least half full?
- Can a reader find your current title, your top skills, and two strong results in a short scan?

If you answer yes to all of these, your resume is the right length, whether it is one page or two.
