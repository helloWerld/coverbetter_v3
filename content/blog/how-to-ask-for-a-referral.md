To ask for a referral, send a short, direct message to someone at the company. Name the exact role, say in one or two sentences why you fit, and make it easy to say yes or no. Attach your resume and a link to the job post so your contact does not have to search. The best referrals come from people who know your work, so start with former coworkers, classmates, and friends before you contact strangers.

## Why a Referral Helps

A referral puts your application in front of a recruiter with a note from someone the company trusts. Many companies have a formal referral program, and some pay employees a bonus when a referred candidate is hired. That means many employees are glad to refer good candidates.

A referral does not guarantee an interview. It gives your application more attention, and it often gets you a faster answer. You still need a resume that fits the job.

## Who to Ask for a Referral

Ask people in this order. The closer they are to your work, the stronger the referral.

1. Former managers and coworkers who saw your work directly.
2. Friends and classmates who know you well.
3. Second-degree contacts, introduced by a friend.
4. People you met at events, in online groups, or in alumni networks.
5. Strangers at the company, after a short conversation.

Search LinkedIn for "[Company]" and filter by your connections. You may find more contacts than you expect.

## What to Prepare Before You Ask

Make the request easy for your contact. Prepare these items first:

- The link to the job post and the job ID.
- Your resume, tailored to the role. This guide shows how to [tailor your resume to a job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description).
- Two or three sentences about why you fit, which your contact can copy into the referral form.
- Your LinkedIn profile, with a clear headline. See these [LinkedIn headline examples](/blog/linkedin-headline-examples) if you need to update it.

## How to Ask for a Referral: The Message Structure

Every good referral request has the same parts:

1. A personal greeting that reminds them how you know each other.
2. The role, with a link.
3. Your fit, in one or two sentences.
4. The direct request.
5. An easy way to say no.
6. Your thanks.

The easy way to say no matters. People feel more comfortable when they do not feel trapped, and you protect the relationship.

## Template 1: A Former Coworker or Friend

> Hi [Name],
>
> I hope things are going well at [Company]. I saw that your team is hiring a [Job Title] ([link to job post]), and I think I am a strong fit. In my current role at [Current Company], I [one result that matches the job post].
>
> Would you be comfortable referring me for this role? I attached my resume, and here is a short summary you can use: "[Two sentences about your experience and fit]."
>
> If it is not a good time, or if you do not feel you know my work well enough, no problem at all. Thank you for considering it.
>
> [Your Name]

## Template 2: A Contact You Do Not Know Well

Start with a question about their work. Ask for the referral in a second message, after they reply.

First message:

> Hi [Name], I am a [your role] interested in the [Job Title] role on your team at [Company]. I saw your post about [topic] and found it useful. Could I ask you two quick questions about what the team works on? Thanks, [Your Name]

Second message, after a short conversation:

> Thanks again for your time, [Name]. Your answers made me even more interested in the role. If you are comfortable with it, would you be willing to refer me? I can send my resume and a short summary to make it easy. If not, I understand completely.

## Template 3: A Request Through a Mutual Friend

> Hi [Friend's Name], I am applying for a [Job Title] role at [Company], and I see that you know [Contact Name] there. Would you be willing to introduce us? I would like to ask [Contact Name] a few questions about the team. I can send you a short note to forward if that helps. Thanks, [Your Name]

## Template 4: A Former Manager

A former manager's referral is often the strongest one. Be direct and specific.

> Hi [Manager Name],
>
> I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company], where you now work. You saw my work on [project] at [Old Company], and that work is close to what this team needs.
>
> Would you be willing to refer me? I attached my resume. I am also happy to talk for 10 minutes if you want an update on what I have done since we worked together.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

## What to Send After They Say Yes

Send everything in one message so your contact can submit the referral in a few minutes:

- The job post link and the job ID.
- Your resume as a PDF.
- Your full name and email, exactly as they should appear in the form.
- A two-sentence summary of your fit.

Ask them to tell you when they submit it, so you can apply at the right time if your application is a separate step.

## Follow-Up and Thanks

If your contact does not answer within about a week, send one short reminder in the same thread. Do not send more than one.

> Hi [Name], I am following up on my note about the [Job Title] role. No pressure if now is not a good time. Thanks, [Your Name]

After they submit the referral, thank them. When the process ends, tell them the result, whether you got the job or not. People like to know how their help turned out.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Asking for a referral with no specific role. "Can you refer me for anything?" is hard to act on.
- Sending a long message about your whole career.
- Asking a stranger for a referral in the first message.
- Asking someone who cannot judge your work to say that you are excellent. Let them describe what they know.
- Applying through the normal portal before the referral, when the company only counts referrals submitted first.
- Forgetting to thank them.

Rules for referrals differ by company. Some companies limit how many referrals one person can submit. Some ask the referrer how well they know you. Ask your contact about the process if you are not sure.

When you are not connected to anyone at the company, a message to the recruiter can be the next best step. This guide on [how to message a recruiter on LinkedIn](/blog/how-to-message-a-recruiter) has templates for that.
