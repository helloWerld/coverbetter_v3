The short answer to how to write a cover letter: match the job post's top two or three requirements with one real result each. Use four short parts: a greeting, an opening that says why you fit, a body with proof, and a close that asks for an interview. Keep it to one page, about 250 to 400 words. Write a new version for each job, because the employer reads it as a sample of how you think and write.

## What a Cover Letter Does

Your resume lists facts. Your cover letter connects those facts to one job. It answers three questions: Why this job? Why this company? Can this person do the work?

A good letter also does things a resume cannot. It explains a career change. It explains a gap. It shows that you understand the problem the team wants to fix. If your resume is the evidence, the cover letter is the short argument that uses the evidence. Keep this in mind as you learn how to write a cover letter: every line must help the argument.

## How to Write a Cover Letter in 7 Steps

### Step 1: Read the job post twice

Read it once for the general idea. Read it again with a pen. Mark every skill, tool, and result that the employer asks for. Then circle the two or three items that appear more than once or appear first. Those are usually the requirements that matter most.

### Step 2: Match each requirement to one result

For each requirement you circled, write one result from your work. A result has an action and an outcome. "Managed social media" is a duty. "Rebuilt our posting schedule and doubled weekly comments in three months" is a result. Use real numbers only when you know them.

| Requirement in the post | Weak match | Strong match |
|---|---|---|
| "Experience with customer onboarding" | "I have onboarding experience." | "I onboarded 40 new accounts last year and wrote the setup guide our team still uses." |
| "Comfortable with data" | "I am good with data." | "I built a weekly sales report in Excel that our manager used in every Monday meeting." |
| "Strong communicator" | "I am a strong communicator." | "I ran the monthly update call for 25 store managers." |

### Step 3: Find the hiring manager's name

Look at the job post, the company's team page, and LinkedIn. A name makes the letter feel written for one person. If you cannot find a name, use "Dear [Team Name] Hiring Team" or "Dear Hiring Manager". Both are acceptable.

### Step 4: Write an opening that says why you fit

Do not start with "I am writing to apply for...". The reader knows that already. Name the job, then give your strongest reason in the first two sentences. You can open with a result, a link between your work and their product, or a short reason you want this role.

Example opening lines:

- "I am applying for the Customer Success Manager role because I have spent four years helping software customers get set up and stay."
- "Last year I cut our support backlog from three weeks to four days. I want to do the same work for [Company]'s growing customer base."
- "I have used [Product] in my own work for two years, and I want to help the team that builds it."

### Step 5: Prove it in one or two body paragraphs

Each body paragraph covers one requirement and one result. Start with the result, then add one sentence of context, then connect it to the new job. Keep each paragraph to three to five sentences. If you have a third strong result, add a short third paragraph. Do not add more than that.

### Step 6: Close with a clear next step

Thank the reader. Say what you want: a conversation. Do not repeat your whole letter in the last paragraph.

Example closing lines:

- "I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help your team with [specific goal]."
- "Thank you for your time. I am available for a call any day next week."

### Step 7: Edit, then check the facts

Read the letter out loud. Cut any sentence that could appear in anyone's letter. Check the company name, the job title, and the hiring manager's name twice. A wrong company name can end a good application.

## The Structure of a Cover Letter

Most cover letters in the US use the same order. Here is what goes in each part and how long it should be.

| Part | What it contains | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Your name, email, phone, city, and LinkedIn URL | 2 to 4 lines |
| Greeting | "Dear [Name]," | 1 line |
| Opening | The job title and your strongest reason to hire you | 2 to 3 sentences |
| Body | One or two results that match the job's main requirements | 1 to 2 paragraphs |
| Close | Thanks and a request for a conversation | 2 to 3 sentences |
| Sign-off | "Sincerely," or "Best regards," and your name | 2 lines |

## A Full Cover Letter Template

This template follows the steps above on how to write a cover letter. Replace every item in square brackets, then cut anything that does not fit the job.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company]. In my [number] years as a [your current role], I have [one result that matches the job's top requirement]. I want to bring that experience to [Company] because [specific reason: the product, the mission, a project, or the team's goal].
>
> At [Current or Past Company], I [action you took] to [problem you fixed]. As a result, [outcome, with a number if you have one]. Your job post asks for [requirement from the post], and this is the work I do best.
>
> I also [second result that matches a second requirement]. This experience would help me [what you would do in the new role] from my first month.
>
> Thank you for your time. I would welcome a conversation about how I can help [Company] with [goal from the job post].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]
> [Email] | [Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]

## Example Cover Letter

Here is the template filled in for a fictional applicant. Use it to see the level of detail, not to copy the words.

> Dear Ms. Rivera,
>
> I am applying for the Operations Coordinator role at [Company]. In three years as an office administrator, I have set up the scheduling, ordering, and vendor systems for a 30-person clinic. I want to do this work for a company that is growing as fast as yours.
>
> When I started at my current clinic, supply orders were made by hand and often arrived late. I moved our orders to a shared spreadsheet with weekly reorder points. After two months, we stopped running out of common supplies. Your job post asks for someone who can "build simple systems that last," and this is the work I enjoy most.
>
> I also train new front-desk staff. I wrote a short guide for our booking software, and new hires now learn the system in their first week.
>
> Thank you for your time. I would welcome a conversation about how I can help your team keep operations simple as you open new locations.
>
> Sincerely,
> Jordan Lee

## Tips That Make a Cover Letter Better

- Use the employer's words. If the post says "client", do not write "customer". This also helps when software scans your application for keywords.
- Write about them more than about you. Each paragraph should connect your result to their need.
- Keep sentences short. A hiring manager reads quickly, and short sentences survive a quick read.
- Match the tone of the company. A bank and a game studio expect different levels of formality.
- Save the file as a PDF unless the employer asks for another format. Name it "[Your Name] Cover Letter [Company].pdf".

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Starting with "To Whom It May Concern". It sounds old and distant. Use a name or "Dear Hiring Manager".
- Repeating the resume line by line.
- Writing only about what you want from the job.
- Using vague words like "hard-working", "team player", or "passionate" with no proof.
- Apologizing for missing skills. Focus on what you can do, and mention a gap only if you have a plan to close it.

## Checklist Before You Send

- The letter names the exact job title.
- The greeting uses a name, or "Dear Hiring Manager".
- The opening gives a reason to keep reading in the first two sentences.
- Each body paragraph has one result that matches one requirement.
- Every number and claim is true.
- The company name is correct everywhere.
- The close asks for a conversation.
- The letter fits on one page.
- You read it out loud once.

When every item is checked, the letter is ready.
