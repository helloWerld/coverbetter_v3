A job search plan is a written list of the roles you want, the companies you target, and the weekly actions that get you interviews. Start with a clear target role, then set weekly goals for applications, networking messages, and interview practice. Block fixed time on your calendar for each activity. Track your numbers every Friday, and change one thing when a step is not working.

## Why You Need a Job Search Plan

Without a plan, a search turns into random scrolling and applying. You can spend hours and still not know what is working. A plan gives each week a clear finish line. It also gives you data, so you can fix the weak step instead of guessing.

A plan also helps with motivation. Replies are not in your control. Your actions are. When you measure the actions, you can see progress even in a slow week.

## Step 1: Define Your Target

Write one or two sentences that describe the job you want. Be specific enough that a friend could forward you a job post.

> I am looking for a [job title] role at a [type or size of company] in [industry]. I want to work [remote, hybrid, or in-office in City]. My minimum salary is [amount], and I can start [date or notice period].

If you have two different targets, such as "project manager" and "operations manager", write one sentence for each. Use a separate version of your resume for each target.

## Step 2: Build a Target Company List

List 20 to 40 companies where you want to work. Include:

- Companies whose products or services you know
- Companies in your industry that are growing
- Companies where you already know someone
- Local employers, if you want to work in-office

For each company, note the careers page, one or two contacts, and any open roles. This list keeps you from applying only to whatever appears on job boards that day.

## Step 3: Set Weekly Goals

Weekly goals are better than daily goals, because some days are full and some are empty. Choose numbers you can reach with quality work.

| Activity | Starter weekly goal | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Tailored applications | 5 to 10 | Each one has a resume and a letter for that job. |
| Networking messages | 5 to 10 | Conversations can lead to referrals and roles that are not posted. |
| Informational calls | 1 to 3 | You learn about teams and get known before a role opens. |
| Follow-ups | All that are due | A polite follow-up can bring a reply. |
| Interview practice | 1 to 2 hours | Practice makes your answers shorter and clearer. |
| Skill work | 1 to 3 hours | A course or project can close a gap you see in job posts. |

These are starting points, not rules. If you are not working, you may do more. If you work full time, start smaller and stay steady.

## Step 4: Block Time on Your Calendar

A goal without a time slot often does not happen. Put each activity on your calendar like a meeting.

### Sample week for someone not working

| Day | Morning (9:00 to 12:00) | Afternoon (1:00 to 3:00) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Find roles, update target list | 2 tailored applications |
| Tuesday | 2 tailored applications | Networking messages |
| Wednesday | Informational call, follow-ups | Skill work |
| Thursday | 2 tailored applications | Interview practice |
| Friday | Follow-ups, weekly review | Free time |

### Sample week for someone working full time

| Day | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 7:00 to 8:00 pm | Find roles and save 3 to 5 posts |
| Tuesday | 7:00 to 8:30 pm | 2 tailored applications |
| Wednesday | Lunch break | 3 networking messages from your phone |
| Thursday | 7:00 to 8:30 pm | 1 or 2 tailored applications |
| Saturday | 9:00 to 11:00 am | Interview practice and weekly review |

Keep at least one day a week free of job search work. Rest helps you stay consistent for a longer search.

## Step 5: Prepare Your Materials Once

Before you start sending applications, prepare the parts you will reuse:

- A master resume with all your results
- A base cover letter with your strongest examples
- A LinkedIn profile that matches your target role
- A 60 to 90 second answer to "tell me about yourself"
- A list of three references who have agreed to help

Then adapt them for each job. Our guides on how to [tailor your resume to a job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description) and [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter) show the steps. Tailoring takes longer than sending the same file everywhere, but a tailored application gives the reader a clear reason to call you.

## Step 6: Track Your Numbers

Use a simple spreadsheet. Add one row for each application, with the company, role, date, source, contact, status, and next follow-up date. Then, every Friday, count these numbers:

- Applications sent
- Networking messages sent
- Replies received
- First interviews
- Later-stage interviews
- Offers

## Step 7: Review Every Week and Fix One Thing

Each Friday, spend 20 minutes on a review. Use the numbers to find the weak step.

| What you see after 3 to 4 weeks | Likely cause | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| Many applications, no replies | Resume not tailored, or the target is too senior or too junior | Rewrite your summary and top bullets for the target role. Check the level of jobs you apply for. |
| Replies from recruiters, but few interviews | The first screen does not go well | Practice your short answers and your salary answer. |
| Interviews, but no offers | Answers lack proof, or weak follow-up | Prepare stories with results, and send thank you notes. |
| Few roles to apply for | The target is too narrow | Add a close job title, a new city, or remote roles. |
| Low energy, missed goals | Goals are too high | Reduce the goals, and keep them steady for two weeks. |

Change one thing at a time. If you change everything at once, you will not know what helped.

## A One-Page Weekly Template

Copy this job search plan template into a note or document, and fill it in every Monday.

> Week of [Date]
>
> Target role: [Job title], [location or remote]
>
> Goals this week:
> - Tailored applications: [number]
> - Networking messages: [number]
> - Informational calls: [number]
> - Interview practice: [hours]
>
> Top 5 companies this week: [Company 1], [Company 2], [Company 3], [Company 4], [Company 5]
>
> Follow-ups due: [Names and dates]
>
> Friday review:
> - What worked: [notes]
> - What did not: [notes]
> - One change for next week: [change]

## Plan for a Long Search

Some searches take a few weeks. Others take many months. The length depends on your field, your level, your location, and the economy. Plan your money and your energy for a longer search than you hope for. If you need income, a part-time or contract role can support you while you keep looking. For questions about unemployment benefits, check your state's official labor department website.

Keep your job search plan simple enough that you follow it in a bad week. A small plan that you follow every week is better than a large plan that you drop after ten days.
