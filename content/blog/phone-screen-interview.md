The best phone screen interview tips are simple. Research the company and role, prepare a 60-second summary of your background, and know your salary range. Have two questions ready, and take the call in a quiet place with your resume in front of you. A phone screen is a short call, often with a recruiter, to check basic fit before the real interviews. Your goal is to sound clear, interested, and qualified, and to move to the next round.

A phone screen is not a deep test of your skills. The screener usually checks a few things. Does your experience match the resume? Does your salary range fit the budget? Can you work the required schedule and location, and do you communicate well? If you prepare for those points, you remove most of the risk.

## What the Screener Wants to Learn

| What they check | Example question | What a good answer shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic fit | "Walk me through your background." | Your experience matches the role. |
| Motivation | "Why are you interested in this role?" | You read the posting and know the company. |
| Logistics | "Are you open to working on site three days a week?" | No surprises later. |
| Pay | "What are your salary expectations?" | Your range fits their budget. |
| Timing | "When could you start?" | Your timeline fits their plan. |
| Communication | Every question | You answer clearly and briefly. |

## Phone Screen Interview Tips: Before the Call

### Research the company and the role

Spend 20 to 30 minutes on research. Read the job post twice. Read the company's About page and recent news. Write down two or three facts you can mention, such as a product, a goal, or a recent change.

### Prepare your short background summary

You will almost always hear some version of "Tell me about yourself." Prepare a 60 to 90 second answer: your current role, one or two relevant results, and why this job is the next step. Our guide to "[tell me about yourself](/blog/tell-me-about-yourself)" has a full structure.

### Know your numbers

Decide your salary range before the call. Research the market rate for the role and location. Many screeners ask about pay early, and some US states and cities require employers to share a pay range in the posting or on request. Our guide on how to answer the [salary expectations question](/blog/salary-expectations-interview-answer) has sample answers.

### Set up your space

- Charge your phone and use a place with a strong signal.
- Choose a quiet room. Close the door and silence other devices.
- Put your resume, the job post, and your notes in front of you.
- Have water, a pen, and paper ready.
- If the call is on a video app with no camera, test the audio first.

One big advantage of a phone screen: the screener cannot see your notes. Use that. Keep a one-page sheet with your summary, your key results, and your questions.

## Common Phone Screen Questions and Sample Answers

### "Walk me through your background."

"I have five years in customer support, the last two as a team lead at a software company. I manage a team of six, and this year we cut our average response time by about a third. I am looking for a role where I can work more on support operations and processes, which is why this role caught my attention."

### "Why are you interested in this role?"

"Your posting focuses on building the support process for a growing team. That is the part of my current job I enjoy most. I also use your product's mobile app, so I know the customer side already."

### "Why are you looking to leave your current job?"

Keep it positive and short. "I have learned a lot in my current role. I want a bigger part in process design, and my current company does not have that kind of role right now."

### "What are your salary expectations?"

"Based on my research for this kind of role in [City], I am looking for a range of [amount] to [amount]. I am flexible depending on the full package. Can you share the budgeted range for this position?"

### "When can you start?"

"I need to give my current employer [notice period, often two weeks in the US] notice. So I could start about [number] weeks after an offer."

### "Do you have any questions for me?"

Always say yes. Ask one or two questions about the role and the process. See the list below.

## Questions to Ask the Screener

The screener knows the process best. Ask questions they can answer.

- "What does the rest of the interview process look like?"
- "What does a successful first few months look like in this role?"
- "What is the most important skill for this role, in the hiring manager's view?"
- "Why is this role open now?"
- "What is the budgeted salary range for this position?"
- "When do you expect to make a decision for the next round?"

Save deeper questions about team culture and strategy for the hiring manager. Our list of [questions to ask in an interview](/blog/questions-to-ask-in-an-interview) has more.

## How to Sound Good on the Phone

The screener only hears your voice. Small habits make a big difference.

- Stand up or sit up straight. Your voice sounds more energetic.
- Smile when you speak. People can hear it.
- Speak a little slower than normal.
- Use the screener's name once or twice.
- Pause before you answer hard questions. A two-second pause is fine.
- Keep most answers under two minutes. If they want more, they will ask.
- Do not eat, chew gum, or type loudly during the call.

## If the Call Surprises You

Sometimes a recruiter calls without warning and asks, "Do you have a few minutes to talk?" If you are not ready, say so politely:

> Hi [Recruiter Name], thank you for calling. I am very interested in the [Job Title] role. I am not in a good place to talk right now. Could we schedule a call for [two specific times]?

A short delay is much better than a rushed, noisy call.

## Phone Screen Prep Checklist

- I read the job post twice and marked the top five requirements.
- I wrote down three facts about the company.
- I practiced my 60 to 90 second background summary out loud.
- I prepared two short stories with results that match the posting.
- I know my salary range and my earliest start date.
- I know my answer about location, remote work, and schedule.
- I wrote two or three questions for the screener.
- I have my resume and notes in front of me.
- My phone is charged, and I am in a quiet place.

## After the Phone Screen

Within a few hours, send a short thank-you email:

> Hi [Screener Name],
>
> Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed learning more about the [Job Title] role and [one specific thing you learned]. I am very interested in moving forward, and I look forward to the next step.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

Then write down what you learned: the process, the timeline, the salary range, and the names of the people you will meet. If you do not hear back by the date they gave you, send a polite follow-up.

Pay transparency rules and common notice periods differ by state and country, so check the rules where the job is located.
