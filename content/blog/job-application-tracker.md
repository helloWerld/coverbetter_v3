A job application tracker is a simple list, usually a spreadsheet, where you record every job you apply for. Each row has the company, role, date, status, contact, and next step, and you update it the same day something changes. A tracker stops you from missing follow-ups and shows which parts of your search work. You can set one up in about 15 minutes.

## Why Use a Job Application Tracker

After a few weeks of applying, it is hard to remember every role. A recruiter calls, and you cannot remember the job post. You apply twice to the same company. You forget to follow up after an interview. A tracker fixes all of these problems.

It also gives you data. When you can see how many applications led to replies, you can tell if the problem is your resume, your targets, or your interviews. That turns a stressful search into a set of small, clear tasks.

## The Columns to Include

Start with the columns in the first table. Add the optional columns only if you will use them.

### Core columns

| Column | What to enter | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Company | The employer's name | [Company] |
| Role | The exact job title from the post | Operations Coordinator |
| Link | The URL of the job post | [URL] |
| Date applied | The day you submitted | 2027-02-01 |
| Source | Where you found it | Company site, LinkedIn, referral |
| Status | The current stage (see below) | Applied |
| Contact | Recruiter or hiring manager name and email | [Name], [Email] |
| Next step | What you will do next | Follow up |
| Next step date | When you will do it | 2027-02-11 |
| Notes | Anything useful | Referred by [Name]; asks for SQL |

### Optional columns

- Salary range from the post
- Location and work type (remote, hybrid, in-office)
- Resume version and cover letter file name
- Interview dates and interviewer names
- Priority (high, medium, low)
- Date closed and reason

Save a copy of each job post as a PDF or in a document, and note where you saved it. Employers often remove posts when the role closes, sometimes before your interview.

## Status Stages

Use a short, fixed list of statuses. A drop-down menu in your spreadsheet keeps the words the same, which makes counting easy.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Saved | You found the role and plan to apply. |
| Applied | You submitted the application. |
| Followed up | You sent a follow-up and are waiting. |
| Screen | You have a recruiter or phone screen scheduled or done. |
| Interview | You are in interviews with the team. |
| Final | You are in the final round. |
| Offer | You received an offer. |
| Accepted | You accepted an offer. |
| Rejected | The employer said no. |
| Withdrawn | You took yourself out of the process. |
| Closed | No reply after your follow-ups, or the post was removed. |

## A Free Job Application Tracker Template

Copy this header row into the first row of a new spreadsheet. Then add one row for each job.

> Company | Role | Link | Date applied | Source | Status | Contact | Next step | Next step date | Resume version | Cover letter file | Notes

Here is an example row, with placeholders:

> [Company] | [Job Title] | [URL] | [Date] | [Source] | Applied | [Recruiter Name], [Email] | Follow up | [Date + 7 to 10 days] | [Resume file name] | [Cover letter file name] | [Key requirements from the post]

### Useful spreadsheet settings

- Freeze the first row so the headers stay visible.
- Add a drop-down list to the Status column.
- Use conditional formatting to color rows with a next step date of today or earlier.
- Sort by next step date each morning.
- Add a second tab for networking contacts, with name, company, date of last contact, and next step.

## Spreadsheet, Board, or App?

| Option | Good for | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel) | Full control, easy counting, free | No reminders unless you set them up |
| Board (Trello, Notion) | Seeing stages as columns, dragging cards | Harder to count and filter |
| Job search app | Reminders, saving posts from your browser | Your data sits in one product; features vary |
| Paper notebook | People who think better on paper | Hard to search and sort |

Pick one and keep all applications in it. Two trackers lead to gaps.

## A Daily and Weekly Routine

### Every day (5 minutes)

1. Add every application you sent today.
2. Update the status of anything that changed.
3. Check the rows where the next step date is today, and do those steps.

### Every week (20 minutes)

1. Count applications, replies, screens, interviews, and offers for the week.
2. Mark old applications as Closed if you have followed up and heard nothing.
3. Look for patterns. Which sources lead to replies? Which roles lead to interviews?
4. Set the goals for next week in your [job search plan](/blog/job-search-plan).

## Follow-Up Rules to Put in Your Tracker

Set the next step date at the moment you add a row. These are common time frames, but every company is different, and a date given by the employer always wins.

| After this | Follow up after |
|---|---|
| Applying online with no contact | 7 to 10 business days, if you can find a contact |
| Applying through a referral | 5 to 7 business days, through the referrer or the recruiter |
| A phone screen | 5 business days, or the day after the date they gave you |
| An interview | Send a thank you email within 24 hours, then follow up after the date they gave |
| A final round | 2 to 3 business days after the date they gave |

Our guide to the [thank you email after an interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview) has templates for the step after each interview.

A short follow-up for an application:

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I applied for the [Job Title] role on [Date] and wanted to confirm that my application arrived. I am very interested in [specific part of the role], and I would be glad to share more about my experience with [relevant skill].
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## Use the Tracker to Improve Your Search

After three or four weeks, your job application tracker can answer these questions:

- Which sources lead to the most replies? Spend more time there.
- Which job titles lead to interviews? Focus on those.
- Which resume version gets more replies? Use it as your new base.
- How long does each company take to reply? Use this to set realistic follow-up dates.

If many applications get no reply, look at how you [tailor your resume to the job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description) and check that your targets match your level.

## Before a Recruiter Calls

When a company calls you, open the row first. Read the saved job post, your notes, and the resume version you sent. Then you can talk about the exact role and the results you used to apply for it.
