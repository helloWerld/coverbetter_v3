Behavioral interview questions ask you to describe how you handled a real situation in the past, such as "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker." The interviewer uses your past actions to predict how you will act in the new job. Answer each one with a specific story: the situation, what you did, and the result. Prepare six to eight stories before the interview, and you can answer most behavioral questions with confidence.

## How to Spot a Behavioral Question

Behavioral questions usually start with one of these phrases:

- "Tell me about a time when..."
- "Give me an example of..."
- "Describe a situation where..."
- "Have you ever...?"
- "What did you do when...?"

They differ from hypothetical questions, such as "What would you do if...?" A behavioral question asks what you did. A hypothetical question asks what you would do. For a behavioral question, always tell a real story.

## The Answer Structure

The most common structure for behavioral answers is the STAR method:

1. Situation: the context, in one or two sentences.
2. Task: your goal or your responsibility.
3. Action: the specific steps that you took.
4. Result: what happened, with a number or a clear outcome if you can.

Spend the most time on the action and the result. Use "I", not "we", so the interviewer knows what you did. This full guide to the [STAR method interview](/blog/star-method-interview) has more examples.

## Behavioral Interview Questions by Skill

Interviewers choose questions that match the skills in the job post. Read the post and expect questions about its main skills.

### Teamwork

1. Tell me about a time you worked on a team to reach a difficult goal.
2. Describe a time you disagreed with a coworker. What did you do?
3. Give me an example of how you helped a teammate who was struggling.
4. Tell me about a time you worked with someone whose style was very different from yours.

### Problem Solving

5. Tell me about a difficult problem you solved at work.
6. Describe a time you found a better way to do a task.
7. Give me an example of a decision you made with incomplete information.
8. Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly.

### Leadership and Initiative

9. Tell me about a time you led a project.
10. Describe a time you took the lead without being asked.
11. Give me an example of how you motivated others.
12. Tell me about a time you had to give difficult feedback.

### Conflict and Pressure

13. Describe a time you worked under a tight deadline.
14. Tell me about a time you dealt with an unhappy customer.
15. Give me an example of a time you had too much work. How did you prioritize?
16. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with your manager.

### Failure and Growth

17. Tell me about a time you made a mistake. What did you learn?
18. Describe a project that did not go as planned.
19. Give me an example of feedback that changed how you work.
20. Tell me about a goal you did not reach.

### Communication

21. Tell me about a time you explained a complex idea to someone outside your field.
22. Describe a time you had to persuade someone to change their mind.
23. Give me an example of a time you had to deliver bad news.
24. Tell me about a presentation that went well or badly.

### Adaptability

25. Tell me about a time your priorities changed suddenly.
26. Describe how you handled a major change at work.
27. Give me an example of a time you worked outside your comfort zone.

### Values and Ethics

28. Tell me about a time you saw someone break a rule. What did you do?
29. Describe a time you put the customer's needs first.
30. Give me an example of a time you admitted a mistake to your manager.

## Sample Answers

Use these as models. Replace every detail with your own true story.

### "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker."

> "On my last team, a coworker and I disagreed about the launch date for a new feature. She wanted to launch on time, and I thought we needed one more week of testing. My task was to find a way forward without delaying the whole team. I asked for a 30-minute meeting and showed her the three bugs I had found. We agreed to launch on time with the feature hidden for most users, and to fix the bugs that week. The launch went well, and we used the same approach for the next two releases."

### "Tell me about a time you made a mistake."

> "In my first year as an analyst, I sent a weekly report to leadership with an error in one formula. The sales numbers for one region were too high. I found it the next morning and told my manager right away. I sent a correction with a short note and checked the other reports for the same error. Then I built a simple check sheet that compares totals before each report goes out. I have not repeated that error since, and two coworkers now use the same check sheet."

A question about a mistake is close to the question about your weakness. This guide to the [greatest weakness interview answer](/blog/greatest-weakness-interview-answer) shows how to make both honest and useful.

## Build Your Story Bank in One Evening

You do not need 30 separate stories. Build a bank of six to eight stories that cover many skills.

1. List your main projects and moments from the last three to five years.
2. For each one, write the situation, task, action, and result in short notes.
3. Mark which skills each story shows: teamwork, leadership, conflict, failure, and so on.
4. Make sure that each skill in the job post has at least one story.
5. Include at least one story about a mistake or a failure.
6. Practice each story aloud until it takes one to two minutes.

| Story | Skills it shows |
|---|---|
| [Project with a tight deadline] | Pressure, prioritizing, teamwork |
| [Disagreement about a decision] | Conflict, communication, influence |
| [Mistake you fixed] | Failure, ownership, process improvement |
| [Project you led] | Leadership, initiative, planning |
| [Difficult customer] | Customer focus, calm under pressure |
| [New skill learned fast] | Adaptability, learning |

Fill in your own stories in the first column.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- A general answer. "I always communicate well with my team" is not a story.
- A hypothetical answer. Do not say what you would do. Say what you did.
- Too much background. The interviewer does not need the full history of the project.
- No result. Always finish with what happened and what you learned.
- Only "we". The interviewer needs to know your part.
- A story that makes a coworker look bad. Describe the situation fairly.
- A made-up story. Follow-up questions will show the gaps.

## Practice Before the Interview

Ask a friend to read questions from the list above in random order. Answer each one with a story from your bank. Record yourself if you can, and check the length and the clarity.

Behavioral interview questions often appear together with other [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions), so prepare both types. With a story bank, each new question becomes a choice of which story to tell, not a test of memory.
