The most common cover letter mistakes are a generic letter, the wrong company name, a copy of the resume, a focus on what you want, and typos. Each one tells the reader that you did not take time with this application. The fix for most of them is the same: write for one job post, use specific examples, and check every detail before you send.

## Why Small Mistakes Matter

A cover letter is a writing sample. The reader judges your care and your communication skills from it, not only your experience. A small error can make a reader doubt the whole application.

The good news: every mistake below is easy to fix once you know it. Use this list as an edit checklist for your next letter.

## Content Mistakes

### 1. A Generic Letter

A letter that could go to any company tells the reader nothing. It also shows that you did not read their job post.

Fix: Name the company, the role, and one specific reason you want this job. Mention at least two requirements from the job post.

### 2. Repeating the Resume

Many letters list every job in order. The reader already has your resume.

Fix: Pick one or two results that match the job post best. Explain what you did and why it matters for this role.

### 3. Talking About What You Want

"This role would be a great step for my career" is about you. The employer wants to know what you give them.

Fix: Rewrite each "I want" sentence as a "you need, and I can do it" sentence.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "This role would help me grow my design skills." | "Your post asks for someone who can redesign the checkout flow. I led a checkout redesign at [Company]." |
| "I am looking for a company with a good culture." | "Your focus on [value] matches how I work. At [Company], I [example]." |

### 4. No Proof

Words like "hardworking", "detail-oriented", and "team player" are claims without proof.

Fix: Replace each claim with a short example. Show, with a fact, that you are detail-oriented.

### 5. False or Exaggerated Claims

An inflated title or an invented number can end your application at the reference check or in the interview.

Fix: Use only facts that you can explain in detail. If you are not sure of a number, describe the result without it.

### 6. Apologizing for What You Lack

"Although I do not have experience in X..." points the reader to your weakest point.

Fix: Lead with what you have. If you must address a gap, do it briefly and connect it to a related skill.

### 7. Too Much Personal Information

Your age, family status, health, or reasons for leaving a past job are rarely needed.

Fix: Keep the letter about the job. Mention a personal detail only if it explains a clear fit, such as a move to the job's city.

## Detail Mistakes

### 8. The Wrong Company Name or Job Title

This happens when you reuse a letter. It is the fastest way to lose a reader.

Fix: Search the letter for every company name and job title before you send it.

### 9. "To Whom It May Concern"

This greeting feels old and distant. It suggests that you did not try to find a name.

Fix: Search the job post, the company website, and LinkedIn for the hiring manager's name. If you cannot find one, use "Dear [Team Name] Hiring Team". This guide on [how to address a cover letter](/blog/how-to-address-a-cover-letter) covers every case.

### 10. Typos and Grammar Errors

Spelling errors are the most avoidable of all cover letter mistakes.

Fix: Run a spell check. Then read the letter aloud, slowly. Read it once more on your phone, because a new screen helps you see errors.

### 11. A Weak First Line

"I am writing to apply for the position of..." wastes the most important line.

Fix: Name the role and give a reason to read on in the same sentence. For example: "Your post for a [Job Title] asks for someone who can [requirement], and I have done that work for [number] years at [Company]."

## Format Mistakes

### 12. Too Long

A letter longer than one page asks too much of a busy reader.

Fix: Keep it to about 250 to 400 words and three or four paragraphs. Follow a clean [cover letter format](/blog/cover-letter-format) with clear spacing.

### 13. A Hard-to-Read Layout

Small fonts, narrow margins, and long blocks of text make the letter hard to read.

Fix: Use a common font at 10 to 12 points, margins of about one inch, and short paragraphs.

### 14. The Wrong File Type or File Name

A file called "letter_final_v3.docx" looks careless.

Fix: Send a PDF unless the employer asks for another format. Name it "[First Name] [Last Name] Cover Letter [Company].pdf".

### 15. A Weak Ending

"I hope to hear from you" ends the letter on a passive note.

Fix: End with a clear next step and a thank you. For example: "I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help [team] with [goal]. Thank you for your time." See more ways to [end a cover letter](/blog/how-to-end-a-cover-letter).

## A Corrected Cover Letter Template

This template avoids every mistake above. Replace each item in square brackets.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Your post for a [Job Title] asks for someone who can [top requirement]. That is the work I have done for [number] years at [Current Company], and it is why I am applying.
>
> At [Current Company], I [specific result that matches the top requirement]. I also [second result that matches another requirement]. Both projects needed [skill from the job post], which I use every day.
>
> I am drawn to [Company] because of [specific detail: a product, a mission, or a team goal]. I would like to bring my experience in [skill] to help your team [goal from the job post].
>
> I would welcome the chance to talk about the role. Thank you for your time.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number] | [Email]

## Final Checklist Before You Send

- The company name and the job title are correct everywhere.
- The greeting uses a name or a specific team.
- The first line names the role and gives a reason to read on.
- The letter mentions at least two requirements from the job post, with an example for each.
- Every claim is true, and you can explain it in an interview.
- The letter is one page or less, with short paragraphs.
- Spelling and grammar are correct. You read it aloud.
- The file is a PDF with a clear name, unless the employer asked for another format.

Fix these cover letter mistakes once, and your letters get better each time you write one.
