A strong tell me about yourself interview answer is a 60 to 90 second summary of your work in three parts. Say who you are now, what you have done that matters for this job, and why you want this role next. Pick one or two results that match the job post. End by connecting your story to this job, so the interviewer has a clear reason to ask the next question.

## Why Interviewers Ask "Tell Me About Yourself"

It is often the first question, and it does several jobs at once. It warms up the conversation. It shows how you summarize information and which parts of your history you think matter. It also gives the interviewer topics for follow-up questions.

The interviewer does not want your life story. They want to know if your past work points toward this job. Your answer should make that connection for them.

## A Tell Me About Yourself Interview Answer Formula

This structure works for most roles and levels.

| Part | What to say | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Present | Your current role or situation, and the main thing you do | 1 to 2 sentences |
| Past | One or two results or experiences that match this job | 2 to 3 sentences |
| Future | Why you want this role, at this company, now | 1 to 2 sentences |

Some people prefer past, present, future. That also works, especially if your early career explains your current direction. Choose one order and use it for every practice run.

## Fill-In Template

Use this template to write your first draft. Then say it out loud and change any words that do not sound like you.

> I am a [current job title or professional label] with [number] years of experience in [area that matches the job]. Right now at [Company or type of company], I [main responsibility, in plain words].
>
> Before that, I [relevant role or experience]. One project I am proud of is [short result: what you did and what changed]. That work taught me [skill the new job needs].
>
> I am looking for a role where I can [what you want to do more of]. That is why this [Job Title] role at [Company] caught my attention. [One sentence about something specific: the product, the team's goal, or something you learned in your research.]

## Tell Me About Yourself Sample Answers

Each sample answer below takes about one minute to say. They are examples of structure and detail. Replace the details with your own.

### Experienced professional

> I am a project manager with seven years of experience in construction and facilities. Right now I manage renovation projects for a regional healthcare group, usually three or four at a time.
>
> Before that, I worked as a site coordinator, so I know how projects look from the field. Last year I led a clinic renovation that had to stay open during the work. We planned the work in phases and kept every patient room open on schedule.
>
> I want to move into a larger organization where I can run bigger projects and help build a project process. Your post mentions a new campus expansion, and that is exactly the kind of work I want to do next.

### Career changer

> I am a high school teacher moving into corporate training. For eight years I have planned lessons, managed groups of 30 people, and adjusted my teaching to many learning styles.
>
> In the last two years I led our school's training for new teachers. I designed a four-week program and ran it for two groups. I also completed a certificate in instructional design so I could learn the tools used in companies.
>
> I am applying for this Learning and Development Specialist role because it uses the parts of teaching I enjoy most. I like to design a program and see adults learn a new skill.

### Recent graduate

> I just finished my degree in accounting at [University]. During my last year I worked part time in the finance office at the university, where I helped with month-end reconciliation and vendor payments.
>
> I also led a student team in a case competition. We analyzed a company's cost structure and presented a plan to a panel of local business owners.
>
> I am excited about this Staff Accountant role because your team trains new staff across audit and tax. I want a strong base before I choose a focus.

### Returning after a gap

> I am an HR generalist with six years of experience in recruiting and employee relations. I took two years away from work for family reasons, and I am now ready to return full time.
>
> In my last role, I handled hiring for a 200-person manufacturing site and rebuilt the onboarding process. During my break, I kept my skills current with an HR certification course.
>
> I am interested in this role because your company is growing its site teams, and that is the type of hiring I know best.

### Internal promotion

> I have been on the support team here for three years. I started on the phones and now handle most of our escalated accounts.
>
> Over the last year I have trained five new agents and written several of the guides in our help center. I enjoy helping people on the team solve problems as much as I enjoy helping customers.
>
> That is why I want the Support Team Lead role. I know our tools and our customers, and I want to help the team grow.

## How to Prepare Your Answer

1. Read the job post and pick the two requirements that matter most.
2. Choose one result from your work for each requirement.
3. Write a draft with the template above.
4. Read the draft aloud with a timer. Cut it until it takes 60 to 90 seconds.
5. Practice it five or six times over two days. Use slightly different words each time.
6. Record yourself once on your phone. Listen for filler words and long pauses.
7. Prepare a 30-second version for short phone screens.

If you already wrote a cover letter for the job, your answer can use the same results. If you [tailored your resume to the job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description), the top bullets of your most recent role are a good place to find them.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Starting with your childhood or your first job.
- Reading your resume line by line.
- Talking for more than two minutes.
- Sharing personal details that have no link to the job.
- Saying negative things about a current or past employer.
- Ending with a weak line like "So, yes, that is me." End with why you want this job.
- Using a generic answer for every interview. Change the "past" and "future" parts for each employer.

## What Comes Next in the Interview

Your tell me about yourself interview answer sets up the next questions. If you mention a project, expect the interviewer to ask about it. Prepare two or three more details about every result you mention: what the problem was, what you did, and what changed.

After the interview, note which parts of your answer got a reaction. Use one of them in your [thank you email after the interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview) to remind the interviewer of your strongest point.
