A follow up email after an interview is a short message. You send it when you have not heard back by the date the employer gave you. Send it one or two business days after that date, or about one week after the interview if they gave no date. Keep it to three or four sentences: thank them, restate your interest, and ask about the timeline.

The follow up email is different from the thank-you email. You send the thank-you within 24 hours of the interview. If you have not sent one yet, start with the guide on the [thank you email after an interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview). This post covers what to do after that, while you wait.

## When to Send a Follow Up Email After an Interview

Timing is the most common question. Use the information the employer gave you.

| Situation | When to send your first follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| They gave you a date ("by Friday") | 1 to 2 business days after that date |
| They gave a range ("in two weeks") | The day after the range ends |
| They gave no timeline | About 5 to 7 business days after the interview |
| You have another offer with a deadline | Now, with a clear and polite note about the deadline |
| They asked you for something (references, a work sample) | Send it within 1 business day, then wait for their date |

At the end of each interview, ask: "What are the next steps, and when can I expect to hear back?" The answer gives you a fair date for your follow-up. It also saves you from guessing.

## What to Put in the Email

A good follow-up has four parts. Each part is one or two sentences.

1. A clear subject line with the job title.
2. A thank-you and a reminder of who you are and when you met.
3. One line that shows you still want the job.
4. A direct, polite question about the timeline or next steps.

Do not repeat your whole interview. Do not list everything you forgot to say. The recruiter needs to see your name, the job, and your question in a few seconds.

### Subject lines that work

- "Following up: [Job Title] interview on [Date]"
- "[Your Name], [Job Title] interview follow-up"
- "Checking in on [Job Title] next steps"

## 5 Follow Up Email Templates

Change the words in brackets. Then read the email aloud once before you send it.

### 1. First follow-up after the expected date

> Subject: Following up: [Job Title] interview on [Date]
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I hope your week is going well. I interviewed for the [Job Title] role with [Interviewer Name] on [Date], and I am still very interested in joining [Company].
>
> You mentioned that the team planned to decide by [Date]. Is there an update on the timeline, or anything else I can send you?
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number]

### 2. Follow-up when they gave no timeline

> Subject: [Your Name], [Job Title] interview follow-up
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you again for the time on [Date] to talk about the [Job Title] role. I enjoyed learning about [specific project or goal from the interview], and the role is a strong fit for my experience in [your skill].
>
> Could you share what the next steps are and when I might hear back?
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

### 3. Second follow-up after no reply

Send this about one week after the first follow-up.

> Subject: Checking in on [Job Title] next steps
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I wanted to check in once more about the [Job Title] position. I know hiring decisions can take time, and I am still interested in the role.
>
> If there is any news on the timeline, I would be glad to hear it.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

### 4. Follow-up when you have another offer

Be honest and calm. Do not use the offer as a threat.

> Subject: Timeline question: [Job Title] at [Company]
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you again for the interviews for the [Job Title] role. [Company] is my first choice, and I want to keep you informed.
>
> I have received another offer, and I need to give an answer by [Date]. Is it possible to learn where I stand in your process before then?
>
> I understand if the timeline cannot change. Thank you for your help.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

### 5. Final follow-up

Use this when you have sent two follow-ups and heard nothing. It closes the loop in a friendly way.

> Subject: [Job Title] at [Company]
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I have not heard back about the [Job Title] role, so I will assume the team has moved forward with other candidates. Thank you for your time and for the chance to meet [Interviewer Name].
>
> If anything changes, or if a similar role opens, I would be glad to talk again.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## How Many Times Should You Follow Up?

Two or three emails in total is a reasonable limit after one interview round. Space them about one week apart. After the final note, stop writing and put your energy into other applications.

Some companies never send a rejection. That is frustrating, but it is common. Silence after three polite emails is an answer, even if it is not a clear one.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing the day after the interview to ask for a decision.
- Sending the same email to several people at the company on the same day.
- Adding a long list of new reasons why you are the best person for the job.
- A tone that blames the company for the delay ("I still have not heard anything").
- Following up on LinkedIn and by email and by phone in the same week.
- Forgetting the job title. Recruiters often hire for many roles at once.

## Add Value When You Can

A follow-up is stronger when it gives the reader something useful. You do not need it every time, but it helps when it is natural.

- A short link to a work sample you mentioned in the interview.
- An answer to a question you did not answer well, in two or three sentences.
- A relevant certificate or result you completed since the interview.

Keep the addition to one or two sentences. Then ask your timeline question.

## Keep Track of Each Follow-Up

When you interview with several companies, the dates mix together fast. Write down the interview date, the person's name, the date they gave you, and each follow-up you send. A simple sheet is enough. The guide on how to build a [job application tracker](/blog/job-application-tracker) has columns you can copy.

Set a reminder for the date of your next follow-up. Then stop checking your inbox every hour. The email you send is short, but the process behind it can take weeks, and other applications need your time while you wait.
