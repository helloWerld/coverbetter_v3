To decline a job offer, reply quickly and thank the person by name. Say clearly that you will not accept, give a short reason if you want, and leave the door open for the future. Send it by email, or call first and then confirm by email. Keep it to four or five sentences. Do not criticize the company or go into detail about another offer.

Declining an offer feels awkward, but it is a normal part of hiring. Recruiters and hiring managers hear "no" often. What they remember is how you said it. A fast, polite answer keeps your reputation strong and lets the company move to its next candidate.

## How to Decline a Job Offer in 6 Steps

1. Decide for sure. Do not decline until you are certain. If you have another offer, accept that one in writing first.
2. Reply quickly. Answer within one or two business days of your decision. Do not let the deadline pass in silence.
3. Choose the channel. Email works for most cases. Call first if you had a close relationship with the hiring manager or recruiter.
4. Thank them. Mention their time and something specific from the process.
5. State the decision clearly. Use words like "I have decided not to accept the offer." Do not leave room for doubt.
6. Close warmly. Say you hope to stay in touch, if you mean it.

## What to Include and What to Leave Out

| Include | Leave out |
| --- | --- |
| Thanks for the offer and their time | Long explanations |
| A clear decision in the first or second sentence | Criticism of the company, the pay, or the people |
| A short, general reason (optional) | The other company's name and salary (unless you want to share) |
| A wish to stay in touch | Promises you do not mean, such as "I will definitely apply again" |
| Your full name and the job title | Any negotiation, if you have decided |

If you still want the job but the pay is too low, do not decline yet. Negotiate first. Our guide on [how to negotiate salary](/blog/how-to-negotiate-salary) shows how.

## Job Offer Decline Email Templates

Replace every item in square brackets. Use the subject line "Job Offer: [Job Title], [Your Name]" so the email is easy to find.

### Template 1: You accepted another offer

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you for offering me the [Job Title] position at [Company]. I enjoyed meeting the team and learning about [specific project or goal].
>
> After careful thought, I have accepted another position that is a better fit for my goals right now. This was a hard decision, because I was impressed by [specific thing you liked].
>
> I hope we can stay in touch, and I wish you and the team the best with [project or goal].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

### Template 2: The role is not the right fit

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you for the offer for the [Job Title] role. I appreciate the time you and the team spent with me.
>
> After thinking about the role in detail, I have decided not to accept. I want a position with more focus on [area, such as client work or data analysis]. I do not think this role is the right fit for me now.
>
> I enjoyed our conversations and hope our paths cross again.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

### Template 3: The pay or benefits do not work

Use this only after you tried to negotiate and the company could not move.

> Dear [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you for the offer for the [Job Title] position and for your help during the process. I also appreciate that you checked if the team could adjust the salary.
>
> I understand that [Company] cannot change the offer at this time. Because of my financial needs, I must decline the offer. I enjoyed meeting the team, and I would be glad to talk again if a role with a different range opens in the future.
>
> Thank you again,
> [Your Name]

### Template 4: You are staying in your current job

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you very much for the offer to join [Company] as [Job Title]. I enjoyed learning about the team and [specific project].
>
> After a lot of thought, I have decided to stay in my current role for now. I am grateful for your time and for the chance to learn about your work.
>
> I hope we can stay connected.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Your Name]

### Template 5: Personal reasons

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you for the offer for the [Job Title] role. Because of personal circumstances, I am not able to accept a new position at this time.
>
> I am sorry that I cannot join the team, and I appreciate your time and understanding. I hope to stay in touch.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

You do not need to explain personal reasons. "Personal circumstances" is enough.

## How to Decline a Job Offer by Phone

A short call can be more personal. Plan what you will say, and keep it under two minutes.

A simple script:

"Hi [Name], thank you for taking my call. I want to thank you again for the offer for the [Job Title] role. I thought about it carefully, and I have decided not to accept. [Short reason.] I really enjoyed meeting you and the team, and I hope we can stay in touch."

If they ask if anything could change your mind, answer honestly. If nothing would change it, say: "Thank you, but my decision is final." After the call, send a short email that confirms the decision in writing.

## How to Decline After You Already Accepted

This is harder, but it happens. Act fast, because the company may have stopped talking to other candidates.

- Call the hiring manager or recruiter first, then send an email.
- Apologize clearly and do not blame the company.
- Keep the reason short.
- Return any equipment or documents they sent you.
- Check your offer letter or contract for terms about signing bonuses, relocation payments, or notice. If you are unsure about your obligations, check the contract or ask a qualified advisor.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I am sorry to tell you that I must withdraw my acceptance of the [Job Title] position. [Short reason, such as "Since I accepted, my circumstances changed, and I cannot start this role."] I know this causes problems for the team, and I apologize. Thank you for your understanding and for the time you invested in me.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

## Mistakes to Avoid

- You wait until the deadline, or you do not answer at all.
- You decline in a text message, unless the recruiter used text for the whole process.
- You give a long list of reasons.
- You say something negative about the company, the team, or the interview.
- You use another offer as pressure when you have already decided to decline.
- You forget to tell the recruiter, if a recruiter managed the process.

## Before You Send

Check that your decision is final, that you named the correct job title and company, and that you spelled the person's name correctly. If you are still choosing between two offers, use our method for [how to compare job offers](/blog/how-to-compare-job-offers) before you reply.

Customs differ in some countries and industries. In some cultures, a phone call is expected for any offer decision, so follow local practice when you know it.
