The hidden job market is the set of jobs that employers fill without a public job posting, or before the posting gets much attention. These roles go to people the company already knows: referrals, former employees, contractors, and candidates who contacted the company first. To reach the hidden job market, choose target companies and build relationships with people who work there. Then ask for advice and referrals before a job is posted.

The term sounds mysterious, but the idea is simple. Hiring takes time and money. When a manager needs someone, they often ask, "Do we know anyone?" before they write a posting. If your name comes to mind at that moment, you compete with few people or with no one.

## Where Hidden Jobs Come From

Not every hidden job is a secret. Many roles become "hidden" for ordinary reasons:

- A manager plans a new role and asks the team for names before HR writes the posting.
- An employee gives notice, and the manager calls a former colleague.
- A team turns a contractor or intern into a full-time employee.
- A company creates a role for a strong person it met, even without an open position.
- An employer posts the job only on its own careers page or in a small industry group.
- A recruiter fills the role from their own contacts before posting it widely.
- A posting goes up, but a referred candidate is already far along in the process.

Many companies also must post some jobs publicly, because of their own policies or because of legal rules for certain employers. The rules vary by employer and location. So a posting can exist even when the company already has a strong internal candidate.

## How to Reach the Hidden Job Market: A 6-Step Plan

### Step 1: Choose 15 to 25 target companies

You cannot build relationships with every employer. Choose companies where you want to work and where your skills fit. Include a mix of large and small companies, because small companies often hire without formal postings.

For each company, write down:

- What they do and which teams match your skills
- Recent news (funding, new products, new offices, new leaders)
- People you know there, or people in your extended network

### Step 2: Map your network

Write a list of everyone you know who could know someone at your target companies. Include former coworkers, former managers, classmates, alumni from your school, friends, neighbors, and people from clubs or volunteer groups. Then use LinkedIn to see who is connected to your targets.

Most hidden jobs come through second-degree connections: the friend of a friend, or a former coworker's new teammate. You do not need a big network. You need a network that you use.

### Step 3: Ask for conversations, not jobs

Contact people for short conversations about their work and their company. People help more when you ask for advice. Use our guide to [informational interview questions](/blog/informational-interview) to prepare.

> Hi [First Name],
>
> I am a [your role] with experience in [skill or area], and [Company] is one of the companies I admire most. I would value your view of what it is like to work on [team or area]. Would you have 15 minutes for a call in the next few weeks?
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

### Step 4: Stay visible to the right people

One conversation is rarely enough. Keep in touch with useful updates every few weeks or months:

- Share an article that relates to something they mentioned.
- Tell them when you acted on their advice.
- Congratulate them on news, such as a promotion or a product launch.
- Comment with real thought on their posts.

When a role opens, the manager remembers the people they heard from recently.

### Step 5: Ask for a referral when the time is right

When you learn about an opening, or when someone says, "We might hire for that soon," ask clearly. Make it easy: send your resume and a few lines they can forward. Our guide on [how to ask for a referral](/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-referral) gives templates.

### Step 6: Contact hiring managers directly

If you have a strong match, you can write to the manager of the team you want to join. Keep the message short and focused on their needs.

> Hi [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I saw that [Company] [recent news, such as launched a new product or opened an office in a city]. Teams in that stage often need [skill you offer]. In my current role at [Current Company], I [one specific result].
>
> If you expect to add people to your team, I would like to talk. My resume is attached.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

Do not send the same message to many managers at one company. Choose the one person whose team fits you best.

## Other Ways Into the Hidden Job Market

| Channel | How it helps | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiters | They fill roles that are never posted publicly. | Connect with recruiters who specialize in your field. See [how to message a recruiter](/blog/how-to-message-a-recruiter). |
| Professional associations | Members often share openings first. | Join one active group. Go to meetings and volunteer. |
| Alumni networks | Alumni are often willing to help people from their school. | Use your school's alumni directory and LinkedIn alumni search. |
| Contract and freelance work | Short projects can turn into full-time roles. | Accept project work at companies you want to join. |
| Industry events | You meet people before they need to hire. | Go with a goal: two real conversations per event. |
| Online communities | Members share leads in Slack groups, forums, and newsletters. | Contribute help first, then ask. |

## A Weekly Routine That Works

A plan you keep is better than a plan that looks big. Here is a simple routine for a full-time search. Reduce the numbers if you are employed.

- Monday: Research two target companies and find two people to contact.
- Tuesday: Send three or four short networking messages.
- Wednesday: Hold one or two conversations.
- Thursday: Send follow-ups and thank-you notes. Update your tracker.
- Friday: Apply to the best posted jobs you found this week.

Track every contact with the date, the topic, and the next step. A simple spreadsheet is enough.

## Mistakes That Close the Door

- You ask for a job in your first message to a stranger.
- You send long messages with your full life story.
- You contact people only when you need something.
- You forget to thank people who helped you.
- You ignore posted jobs completely and wait for hidden ones.
- You do not tell your network what kind of job you want. Be specific: the role, the industry, and the location.

The hidden job market takes more time than clicking "Apply." But each conversation teaches you about real roles, and each new contact can lead to the next one. Over weeks, those small steps add up.

In some countries and industries, networking customs differ, for example in how direct a first message can be. Adjust your tone to local practice.
