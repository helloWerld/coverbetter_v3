A job search during the holidays still works. Hiring often slows in late December, but it rarely stops, and some teams want to fill roles before a new budget year. Keep applying to roles that are open now, and use quiet weeks and social events to reconnect with people. Prepare your materials so you can move fast in January, and expect slower replies.

## What Changes in December

Hiring depends on people, and many people take time off in late December. Interviews can take longer to schedule. Decisions can wait for one person to come back. A recruiter may reply in two weeks instead of two days.

Some things do not change. Jobs are still posted. Teams that need a person still need one. Some companies want to finish hiring before the year ends, and others start new budgets in January, which can open new roles. The pattern varies by company, industry, and country, so do not assume that every employer is closed.

| Usually slower | Usually still active |
|---|---|
| Interview scheduling in the last two weeks of December | New job posts, especially early in the month |
| Final decisions that need several approvals | Recruiters who are planning January hiring |
| Replies to cold applications | Retail, delivery, and hospitality roles with seasonal demand |
| Offers that need a budget sign-off | Conversations with people you already know |

## Why a Job Search During the Holidays Can Help You

Some job seekers stop until January. If you keep going, your application may face fewer others for the same role. You also have time for work that a busy month does not allow. Rewrite your resume, build a target company list, and reconnect with people.

Holiday events also put you in the same room as friends, family, and old colleagues. Many of them want to help but do not know what you are looking for. A clear one-sentence answer can turn a casual chat into an introduction.

## A Week-by-Week Holiday Plan

### Week 1 (early December): apply and schedule

- Apply to roles that are open now. Do not wait for January.
- If you are in an interview process, ask about the timeline: "Do you expect to make a decision before the holidays, or in January?"
- Send applications early in the week, when people are still at work.

### Week 2 (mid-December): reconnect

- Send short holiday messages to former managers, colleagues, and classmates.
- Update your LinkedIn profile and your headline.
- Ask two or three people for a short call in early January.

### Week 3 (around Christmas): prepare

- Update your master resume with every result from this year.
- Write a base cover letter that you can adapt quickly.
- Build a list of 20 to 30 target companies.
- Practice your answer to "tell me about yourself" and two or three common questions.

### Week 4 (between Christmas and New Year's Day): plan

- Check which of your target companies posted new roles.
- Write a job search plan for January: how many applications, how many conversations, and which days.
- Rest. A short break helps you start January with energy.

## Holiday Networking Messages

A holiday message is a good reason to contact someone you have not spoken to in a while. Keep it short, warm, and easy to answer. Do not attach your resume to the first message.

### Message to a former colleague

> Hi [Name],
>
> I hope you are well and have a good break planned. I was thinking about our time on [project or team] at [Company], and I wanted to say hello.
>
> I am now looking for a [type of role] role in [industry or city]. If you hear of anything, or know someone I should talk to, I would be grateful. Either way, I hope we can catch up in the new year.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

### Message to someone at a target company

> Hi [Name],
>
> I hope your December is going well. I have followed [Company]'s work on [product, project, or announcement] for some time, and I am interested in roles on the [team name] team.
>
> Would you be open to a 15-minute call in early January? I would like to learn what the team values in new hires.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

### What to say at a holiday party

When someone asks what you do, give one clear sentence:

> I am a [job title] with [number] years in [area], and I am looking for my next role in [type of company or industry]. If you know anyone at [company or type of company], I would love an introduction.

## How to Handle Slow Replies

Silence in December usually means people are away. It does not always mean no.

- Write down the date of each application and each interview.
- If an interviewer gave you a date, wait two or three working days after it before you follow up.
- If you had an interview, send a [thank you email after the interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview) within 24 hours, even if the office closes the next day.
- For applications with no response, wait until the second week of January to follow up.

Here is a short follow-up for an interview process:

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I hope you had a good holiday. I wanted to check in on the [Job Title] role. I am still very interested and happy to share anything else you need.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## Get Your Materials Ready for January

January can bring a wave of new postings and new applicants. Use the quiet days of your job search during the holidays to make your materials fast to adapt.

- Update your master resume with every project and result from the year.
- Write three or four strong bullets for each role that you can reorder for each job. Our guide to [tailor your resume to a job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description) shows the method.
- Draft a base cover letter, so each new letter takes minutes instead of an hour. Use the structure in [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter).
- Collect work samples, references, and certificates in one folder.
- Check that your email address, voicemail, and LinkedIn profile look professional.

## Take Care of Yourself

A job search during the holidays can feel lonely when people around you are celebrating. Questions from family about your search can also be hard. Prepare a short, calm answer, such as: "I am looking for a role in [area]. I have some conversations going." Then change the subject if you want to.

Set a small daily goal, such as one application or one message. Small, steady steps are easier to keep during a busy month than big plans. Count the work you control: messages sent, applications finished, materials improved. Replies are not in your control, especially in December.

## Holiday Job Search Checklist

- Applications sent for roles that are open now
- Interview timelines confirmed before the break
- Holiday messages sent to 5 to 10 people
- LinkedIn profile and headline updated
- Master resume updated with this year's results
- Base cover letter ready
- Target company list started
- January plan written with weekly goals
