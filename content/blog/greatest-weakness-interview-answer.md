To answer "What is your greatest weakness?", name one real weakness that is not a core skill for the job. Give a short example of how it affected your work. Then explain what you do to improve it, with proof of progress. Keep the answer to about one minute. The interviewer wants to see self-awareness and a plan, not a perfect person.

This guide gives you a simple formula, a list of weaknesses that work, weaknesses to avoid, and 10 sample answers you can adapt.

## Why Interviewers Ask About Your Weakness

The question is not a trap. The interviewer usually wants to learn three things:

- Do you know yourself well enough to name a real weakness?
- Are you honest, or do you give a rehearsed non-answer?
- Do you take action to improve?

A good answer shows maturity. A dodge, such as "I work too hard", tells the interviewer that you did not take the question seriously.

## How to Answer "What Is Your Greatest Weakness?"

Use these four parts. Each is one or two sentences.

1. Name the weakness. Be specific.
2. Give a short example of when it showed up.
3. Explain what you are doing about it.
4. Show the progress so far.

Template:

> One area I am working on is [specific weakness]. For example, [short situation where it showed up]. To improve, I [specific action, such as a course, a tool, a habit, or asking for feedback]. Since then, [result or progress].

## How to Pick the Right Weakness

Good choices are real, specific, and not central to the job.

| Choose a weakness that... | Avoid a weakness that... |
| --- | --- |
| Is real and you can give an example | Is a fake strength ("I care too much") |
| Is a skill or habit you can improve | Is a fixed trait ("I am not a people person") |
| Is not a core requirement of the job | Is a main task in the job post |
| You are already working on | You have done nothing about |
| Is professional | Is personal or private |

Read the job post again before you choose. If the role is mostly public speaking, do not choose public speaking. If the role is mostly writing code alone, public speaking may be a fine choice.

## Weaknesses That Often Work

- Delegating tasks (for people moving into leadership).
- Public speaking or presenting to large groups.
- Saying yes to too many requests.
- Giving direct feedback to peers.
- A specific tool or software you have not used much.
- Asking for help too late.
- Being too detailed in written updates.
- Impatience with slow processes.
- Lack of experience in a specific area, such as budgeting.

## Weaknesses to Avoid

- "I am a perfectionist", "I work too hard", or "I care too much", without a real example.
- "I do not have any weaknesses."
- Anything that is a core skill of the job.
- Lateness, missing deadlines, or not following through, unless you have a strong story of change.
- Personal matters, health information, or anything you do not want to discuss.
- Problems with people, such as "I do not get along with coworkers".

## 10 Sample Answers

Change the details so every part is true for you.

### 1. Delegating

> I have found delegating hard. When I first led a small team, I kept the hardest tasks for myself, and I worked late while others waited for work. I started to plan the week with the team on Monday and assign tasks based on each person's goals. Now I hand off most of the routine work, and two team members have grown into areas I used to cover.

### 2. Public speaking

> Presenting to large groups makes me nervous. In my last role, I avoided presenting our quarterly results and let my manager do it. I joined a local speaking group and asked to present at our team meeting every month. I still prepare a lot, but I now present to the full department with confidence.

### 3. Saying yes too often

> I tend to say yes to too many requests. Last year, I took on three side projects and the quality of my main work slipped. Now I check my priorities with my manager before I accept new work, and I give a clear timeline when I say yes.

### 4. A specific tool

> I have less experience with Tableau than with Excel. I can build basic dashboards, but I am slower than I want to be. I am taking an online course and rebuilding one of my old Excel reports in Tableau each week.

### 5. Giving direct feedback

> I have avoided giving direct feedback to peers because I did not want to cause conflict. That meant small problems grew. I started to use a simple structure: what I saw, the effect, and a question. It has made those conversations shorter and less awkward.

### 6. Asking for help late

> I sometimes try to solve problems alone for too long. On one project, I spent two days on a bug that a teammate fixed in an hour. Now I set a time limit. If I am stuck after an hour or two, I ask.

### 7. Too much detail

> My written updates can be too long. A manager once told me she read only the first paragraph. Now I put the main point and any decision needed in the first two lines, and the details below.

### 8. Impatience

> I can get impatient when a process moves slowly. Early in my career, I pushed for fast decisions without understanding why the process existed. Now I ask why a step is there before I suggest a change, and my suggestions are more often accepted.

### 9. Lack of budget experience

> I have not yet owned a budget. I have tracked spending for my projects, but someone else made the final decisions. I asked to shadow our finance partner during this year's planning, and I am taking a short course on budgeting basics.

### 10. Career changer

> Because I am moving from teaching into project management, I have less experience with formal project tools. I completed a certificate course and used Asana to run a volunteer event with 20 people. I am still learning, but I am comfortable with the basics.

## How to Deliver Your Answer

- Keep a calm, matter-of-fact tone. It is a normal question.
- Spend most of the time on what you did to improve.
- End on the progress, not on the weakness.
- Do not go back and add more weaknesses unless they ask.

Practice the answer aloud with the rest of your interview prep. The guide on [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions) covers the questions that often come before and after this one. If the interviewer asks for a story about a failure instead, use the [STAR method interview](/blog/star-method-interview) structure.

## A Final Check

Before the interview, read your answer and ask:

- Is the weakness real and specific?
- Is it outside the core of this job?
- Did I give a real example?
- Did I describe a real action and real progress?
- Can I say it in about one minute?

If the answer to each question is yes, you are ready for one of the most common interview questions.
