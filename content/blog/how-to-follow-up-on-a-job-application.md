To follow up on a job application, wait about one to two weeks after you apply. Then send a short, polite email or LinkedIn message to the recruiter or hiring manager. Say which job you applied for and when, add one line about why you fit, and ask whether they need anything else from you. If you hear nothing, send one more message about a week later, then stop.

A follow-up does not guarantee a reply. It does put your name in front of a person, which a submitted form cannot always do. This guide covers timing, who to contact, what to say, and templates you can copy.

## When to Follow Up on a Job Application

Waiting too short a time looks impatient. Waiting too long means the role may already be filled. Use the job post and your own situation to choose a date.

| Situation | When to follow up |
| --- | --- |
| Standard online application | About 1 to 2 weeks after you apply |
| The post shows a closing date | A few business days after that date |
| You applied through a referral | Ask your contact to check in after about 1 week |
| You already met someone from the company | After about 1 week, to that person |
| The post says "no calls or emails" | Do not follow up about that application |

Apply early when you can. Many teams start to review applications while the post is still open, so an early application and a polite follow-up work well together.

## Who to Contact

The best person is the one who will make or influence the decision. Try these in order:

1. The recruiter or hiring contact named in the job post.
2. The recruiter for that team. Search LinkedIn for "[Company] recruiter" or "talent acquisition".
3. The hiring manager. The job post often names the team, which helps you guess the manager's title.
4. A person you know at the company, who can pass your name along.

Do not message the CEO or five people at once. One or two well-chosen contacts are enough.

### How to find an email address

Look at the job post, the careers page, and the contact's LinkedIn profile. Some people list an email there. If you cannot find an address, send a LinkedIn message instead. Do not guess email addresses and send to many versions.

## What to Say in a Follow-Up

Keep the message short. Four parts are enough:

1. The job title and the date you applied.
2. One sentence about your strongest match for the role.
3. A polite question: is there anything else they need, or what is the timeline?
4. A thank-you.

Use a subject line that makes the purpose clear, such as "Application for [Job Title], [Your Name]".

## Follow-Up Email Template After You Apply

> Subject: Application for [Job Title], [Your Name]
>
> Hi [Recruiter or Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I applied for the [Job Title] role at [Company] on [Date], and I wanted to confirm my interest. My experience in [top skill from the job post], including [one short proof], fits what the team is looking for.
>
> Is there anything else I can send to help with your review? I would also be glad to hear about the timeline when you have one.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number] | [LinkedIn URL]

## LinkedIn Message Template

LinkedIn connection notes have a short character limit, so keep this version brief.

> Hi [Name], I recently applied for the [Job Title] role at [Company]. I have [number] years of experience in [skill], and I am very interested in the team's work on [project or product]. I would be glad to connect and answer any questions.

If you are already connected, you can send a longer direct message, similar to the email above.

## Second Follow-Up Template

Send this about one week after the first message, only if you had no reply.

> Subject: Re: Application for [Job Title], [Your Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I wanted to check in once more on my application for the [Job Title] role. I am still very interested and would welcome the chance to talk.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]

Reply in the same email thread as your first message, so the reader sees the history.

## Should You Call Instead?

Email is the better choice in most cases. The reader can answer when they have time, and there is a written record. A phone call can make sense for small local businesses, or for trades and retail jobs where the manager is often on site. If you call, say your name, the job, and the date you applied in the first few seconds. Then ask when a good time is to check back.

Walking in to follow up can work for a local shop or restaurant. It rarely makes sense for an office job or a large company.

## What to Do Before You Follow Up

A follow-up works best when the application itself was strong. Before you write, check these points:

- Your resume uses the words of the job post. Many companies search their applicant tracking system for those words. The guide on [what an applicant tracking system is](/blog/what-is-an-applicant-tracking-system) explains how that search works.
- You sent a cover letter if the form allowed one.
- You answered every application question carefully.
- You have a record of the job title, the date, the link, and any contact names.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Following up two days after you apply.
- Asking "Did you get my application?" The automatic confirmation email usually answers that.
- Attaching your resume again without being asked, in a large file.
- Writing a long message that repeats your cover letter.
- Sending the same text to several people at the company.
- A tone that sounds annoyed about the wait.

## Track Every Follow-Up

When you apply to many jobs, you will forget who you contacted and when. Keep a column for "follow-up date" and another for "follow-up sent" in your tracker. The guide on building a [job application tracker](/blog/job-application-tracker) has a full set of columns.

If you get an interview, the rules change a little. After the interview, you send a thank-you, and then you follow up based on the timeline they gave you. The guide on the [follow up email after an interview](/blog/follow-up-email-after-interview) covers that stage.

## When to Stop

After two follow-ups with no reply, stop writing about that application. Note it in your tracker as "no response", and set it aside. Sometimes a company contacts you weeks later. More often, the best next step is the next application.
