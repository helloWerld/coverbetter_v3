The standard US cover letter format is a one-page business letter with six parts. In order, they are your contact details, the date, the employer's details, a greeting, the body, and a sign-off. Use 1-inch margins, a plain font at 10.5 to 12 points, and single spacing. Keep the body to three or four short paragraphs (about 250 to 400 words), and save it as a PDF.

## The Parts of a Cover Letter in Order

Each part has a fixed place. A reader who sees the usual order can find your name, the job, and your main point fast.

| Part | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Your header | Full name, city and state, phone, email, LinkedIn or portfolio URL | Jane Smith, Denver, CO, (555) 555-0100, jane@email.com |
| 2. Date | The date you send it, written out | December 7, 2026 |
| 3. Employer details | Hiring manager's name and title, company, city and state | Ms. Ana Torres, Hiring Manager, [Company], Austin, TX |
| 4. Greeting | "Dear" and the person's name, then a colon or comma | Dear Ms. Torres: |
| 5. Body | Opening, one or two proof paragraphs, a close | 3 to 4 paragraphs |
| 6. Sign-off | "Sincerely," and your full name | Sincerely, Jane Smith |

You do not need your full street address. City and state are enough for most US applications. Leave out personal details such as your date of birth, a photo, or your marital status. Some other countries expect a photo or more personal details, so check local norms if you apply outside the US.

## Cover Letter Format Settings

These settings work for almost every office job. Use them unless the employer gives different instructions.

| Setting | Recommended |
|---|---|
| Page size | US Letter (8.5 x 11 inches) |
| Margins | 1 inch on all sides (0.75 inch is OK if you need space) |
| Font | A plain font such as Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Georgia, or Garamond |
| Font size | 10.5 to 12 points for the body, up to 14 to 16 for your name |
| Line spacing | Single (1.0 to 1.15) |
| Paragraph spacing | One blank line between paragraphs |
| Alignment | Left-aligned, not justified |
| Length | One page, about 250 to 400 words |
| File type | PDF, unless the employer asks for DOCX |

Use the same font as your resume. Do not use more than two fonts. Avoid colors except for one accent in your name or header, and keep the body text black or dark gray.

## How to Format the Body

The body is where your argument lives. A clear cover letter format makes the argument easy to follow.

### Opening paragraph (2 to 3 sentences)

Name the job title and give your main reason to be considered. If someone referred you, name them here.

### Proof paragraphs (1 or 2 paragraphs, 3 to 5 sentences each)

Each paragraph takes one requirement from the job post and shows one result from your work. Start the paragraph with the result. Do not use bullet points for the whole body. A short list of two or three results is fine if the rest of the letter is in paragraphs.

### Closing paragraph (2 to 3 sentences)

Thank the reader and ask for a conversation. You can say when you are available.

For a full method to write each paragraph, read our step-by-step guide on [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter).

## A Cover Letter Format Template

Copy this structure into a document. Replace every item in square brackets.

> [Your Full Name]
> [City, State] | [Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn URL]
>
> [Month Day, Year]
>
> [Hiring Manager Name]
> [Hiring Manager Title]
> [Company]
> [City, State]
>
> Dear [Mr./Ms./Mx. Last Name or First and Last Name]:
>
> I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company]. [One sentence with your strongest reason: a result, years of relevant experience, or a link to their product or mission.]
>
> [Proof paragraph 1: a result that matches the job's top requirement. Start with the result. Add one sentence of context. End with how it helps the new team.]
>
> [Proof paragraph 2: a result that matches a second requirement. Keep it shorter than the first.]
>
> Thank you for your time and consideration. I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help [Company] with [goal from the job post].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Full Name]

If you do not know the hiring manager's name, leave out the name and title lines and write "Dear Hiring Manager:".

## Format for Email and Online Forms

The format changes a little when the letter is not a file.

### Cover letter in an email body

- Put the job title and your name in the subject line, for example "Application: [Job Title], [Your Name]".
- Skip the date and the employer's address.
- Start with the greeting.
- Put your contact details under your name at the end.
- Attach your resume as a PDF.
- Keep it a little shorter than a file letter, because people read email on phones.

> Subject: Application: [Job Title], [Your Name]
>
> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> [Opening paragraph.]
>
> [One or two proof paragraphs.]
>
> [Closing paragraph.]
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Full Name]
> [Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]

### Cover letter in an online form

Many application forms have a plain text box. Formatting such as bold or bullets may not survive. Write the letter in a plain text editor first, then paste it. Start with the greeting. Keep a blank line between paragraphs. Check the result in the form before you submit.

### Cover letter as an attachment

If the form lets you upload a file, upload a PDF. A PDF keeps your layout the same on every device. Some older systems ask for DOCX. Follow the instructions in the job post when they name a file type.

## Greeting and Sign-Off Formats

| Situation | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| You know the name | Dear Ms. Torres: or Dear Ana Torres: | Sincerely, |
| You know the team | Dear Marketing Hiring Team: | Best regards, |
| You know nothing | Dear Hiring Manager: | Sincerely, |
| Informal company or email | Hi Ana, | Best, |

A colon after the greeting is the most formal choice. A comma is fine for most companies and for email. Avoid "To Whom It May Concern", because it sounds distant.

If you are not sure of a person's gender, use their full name: "Dear Jordan Lee:".

## Common Formatting Mistakes

- More than one page. Cut words before you cut margins.
- Tiny margins or a font under 10 points to fit more text.
- Justified text, which can make uneven gaps between words.
- A different font from your resume.
- Text boxes, columns, or tables, which some systems read in the wrong order.
- A file name like "CoverLetter_final_v3.docx".
- An old date or the wrong company name from a past letter.
- A header that does not match your resume.

## Final Format Checklist

- One page, 250 to 400 words in the body
- 1-inch margins, single spacing, left-aligned
- Same font and header as your resume
- Correct date and company details
- Greeting with a name, or "Dear Hiring Manager"
- Short paragraphs with one blank line between them
- Sign-off with your full name
- Saved as a PDF with a clear file name
