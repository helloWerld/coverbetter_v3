Five of the most common interview questions are "Tell me about yourself", "Why do you want this job?", and "What are your strengths and weaknesses?". The other two are "Tell me about a challenge you solved" and "Why are you leaving your current job?". Most interviews use some version of these, plus a few role-specific questions. Prepare a short, true story for each one, and you can answer most of what you will hear.

This guide lists 20 common interview questions. For each one, you get what the interviewer wants to learn and a simple way to answer.

## How to Prepare for Common Interview Questions

Do not write 20 separate scripts. Instead, prepare five to eight stories from your work, school, or volunteer life. Each story should show a skill the job needs. Then match stories to questions.

For each story, note four things:

1. The situation and your role.
2. The task or problem.
3. The actions you took.
4. The result, with a number if you have a true one.

That is the STAR structure. The guide on the [STAR method interview](/blog/star-method-interview) technique shows how to build each story step by step.

Then practice aloud. Say each answer two or three times. Record yourself on your phone once and listen back. You will hear filler words and long parts that you can cut.

## Questions About You

### 1. Tell me about yourself.

The interviewer wants a short summary that connects your past to this job. Use present, past, and future: what you do now, what you did before that matters, and why this role is the next step. Keep it to about one minute. The full guide on the [tell me about yourself](/blog/tell-me-about-yourself) answer has examples.

### 2. What are your greatest strengths?

Name one or two strengths that the job post asks for. Give one short example for each. "I am good at explaining technical things to customers. At [Company], I rewrote our setup guide, and support tickets about setup dropped."

### 3. What is your greatest weakness?

Name a real weakness that does not block the core of the job. Then say what you do to improve it. Avoid fake weaknesses such as "I work too hard".

### 4. How would your coworkers describe you?

Pick two or three words and back each with a short proof. If you have a real quote from a review or a manager, you can use it.

### 5. What are you most proud of in your career?

Choose a result that is relevant to the new job. Tell it as a short STAR story, and say why it matters to you.

## Questions About the Job and the Company

### 6. Why do you want to work here?

The interviewer wants to see that you did research and that you chose them on purpose. Name something specific: a product, a customer group, a value, or a project. Then link it to your skills.

### 7. Why are you interested in this role?

Talk about the work itself. Point to two or three tasks in the job post that you enjoy and do well.

### 8. What do you know about our company?

Know the basics: what they sell, who their customers are, and one recent piece of news from their website or press page. Say it in your own words.

### 9. Why should we hire you?

Summarize your fit in three or four sentences. Match their top needs from the job post with your proof. End with how you will help in the first months.

### 10. Where do you see yourself in five years?

They want to know if your goals fit the role. Talk about the skills you want to grow and the kind of work you want to own. You do not need a job title.

## Questions About Your Experience

### 11. Walk me through your resume.

Go in time order, briefly. Spend the most time on the roles most like this one. Explain each move in one sentence.

### 12. Why are you leaving your current job?

Stay positive and short. Focus on what you want next, not what you dislike now. "I have learned a lot in support, and I want to move into a role with more product work."

### 13. Why is there a gap in your employment?

Give a short, honest reason. Then say what you did to stay current and why you are ready now. You do not need to share private details.

### 14. Tell me about a time you made a mistake.

Pick a real mistake with a clear lesson. Spend most of the answer on how you fixed it and what you changed after.

## Behavioral Questions

Behavioral questions start with "Tell me about a time..." or "Give me an example of...". Use a STAR story for each.

### 15. Tell me about a challenge you solved.

Choose a problem with real stakes. Show your thinking, not only the result.

### 16. Tell me about a conflict with a coworker.

Show that you listened, stayed calm, and focused on the work. Do not blame the other person. End with how the relationship or the process improved.

### 17. Tell me about a time you led a project or a team.

Leadership can be informal. Training a new hire or running a small process change counts.

### 18. Tell me about a time you had a tight deadline.

Show how you set priorities, what you said no to, and how you kept people informed.

## Questions About Logistics

### 19. What are your salary expectations?

Research the pay range for the role and the location first. Many US job posts now show a range, and some states require it. You can give a range based on your research, or ask what range they have budgeted. Check your local rules on salary history questions, because they differ by state and city.

### 20. Do you have any questions for us?

Always say yes. Prepare three to five questions about the work, the team, and how they measure success. Do not ask about anything that the job post already answers.

## A Quick Table: What Each Question Tests

| Question | What the interviewer is checking |
| --- | --- |
| Tell me about yourself | Can you summarize your fit clearly? |
| Why do you want to work here? | Did you research us and choose us on purpose? |
| Greatest weakness | Are you self-aware and willing to improve? |
| Why are you leaving? | Is there a problem we should know about? |
| Tell me about a conflict | Can you work with people when it is hard? |
| Salary expectations | Are we in the same range? |
| Questions for us | Are you seriously interested in the job? |

## Practice Plan for the Week Before

- Day 1: Read the job post twice. List the top five skills it asks for.
- Day 2: Write your five to eight stories in STAR notes.
- Day 3: Match stories to the 20 questions above. Fill any gaps.
- Day 4: Practice aloud. Time each answer.
- Day 5: Do a mock interview with a friend, or record yourself.
- Day before: Review your notes, prepare your questions, and plan your route or test your video setup.

## After the Interview

Write down the questions you were asked while you still remember them. Note which answers went well and which did not. That list makes your next interview easier.

Then send a short thank-you within 24 hours. The guide on the [thank you email after an interview](/blog/thank-you-email-after-interview) has templates you can copy.
