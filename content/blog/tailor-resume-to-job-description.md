To tailor your resume to a job description, find the skills the employer asks for most, then put those items first, in the employer's words. Rewrite your summary for the role, move the most relevant bullets to the top, and update your skills section. Only include skills you really have. With a master resume, the whole process takes about 30 minutes.

## Why Tailoring Your Resume Works

A hiring manager reads your resume with one question: can this person do this job? A general resume makes them search for the answer. A tailored resume puts the answer at the top of each section.

Many employers also use an applicant tracking system (ATS) to collect and sort applications. Some of these systems let recruiters search or rank resumes by keywords. How they do this varies by product and by employer. Using the same words as the job post helps in both cases: software can find the match, and a person can see it quickly.

## Before You Start: Build a Master Resume

A master resume is a private document that lists everything you have done. It has every job, every project, every tool, and every result you can remember. It can be three or four pages long, because nobody else reads it.

When you tailor your resume to a job description, you copy the master and cut it down. This is faster than adding new material each time. It also stops you from forgetting a good result that you have not used in a while.

## How to Tailor Your Resume to a Job Description: 6 Steps

### Step 1: Pull the key terms from the job post (5 minutes)

Copy the job description into a document. Highlight:

- Job titles and role names
- Hard skills and tools (for example, "Salesforce", "SQL", "budget forecasting")
- Certifications and licenses
- Results the employer wants (for example, "reduce churn" or "launch new markets")
- Soft skills that appear more than once

Then make a short list of the 8 to 12 terms that appear most or appear first. Those are your target terms.

### Step 2: Mark what you already match (5 minutes)

Open your master resume. For each target term, find the place where you show that skill. Mark it. If you have the skill but use a different word, note the employer's word. For example, you wrote "client onboarding" but the post says "customer implementation". If both describe the same work, use their word.

If you do not have a skill, do not add it. You can mention that you are learning it in a cover letter instead.

### Step 3: Rewrite your summary (5 minutes)

Your summary sits at the top of the page. It is the first thing a recruiter reads. Write two or three sentences that name the role, your years of relevant experience, and the two skills the employer needs most.

Fill-in template:

> [Job title or professional label] with [number] years of experience in [area that matches the job]. Known for [top skill from the post] and [second skill from the post]. Most recently [one result that matches the employer's main goal].

Before and after:

| Version | Summary |
|---|---|
| General | "Marketing professional with experience in many channels and a passion for growth." |
| Tailored | "Email marketing manager with 5 years in B2B software. Skilled in lifecycle campaigns and HubSpot automation. Most recently grew trial-to-paid conversion with a new onboarding email series." |

### Step 4: Reorder and rewrite your bullets (10 minutes)

In each job, put the bullets that match the target terms at the top. A reader who skims sees the top of each role first. Make those bullets count.

Then rewrite the top bullets so they use the employer's terms. Keep the structure: action, task, result.

- Before: "Responsible for social media accounts."
- After (for a community role): "Grew our online community to 12,000 members by running weekly live Q&A sessions and moderating the forum."

Cut bullets that have no link to the new job, unless you need them to show a full picture of the role. Older jobs can have fewer bullets.

### Step 5: Update your skills section (3 minutes)

List the target skills that you have, in the same order of importance as the job post. Use the exact names of tools. Write "Google Analytics 4", not "web analytics software". Remove skills that do not matter for this job, so the important ones are easy to see.

### Step 6: Check the match (2 minutes)

Read the job post again, then read your resume. Ask:

- Can a reader find each of the top requirements in 10 seconds?
- Does the summary name this role or one close to it?
- Are the first two bullets in my most recent job relevant?
- Did I use the employer's words for skills I really have?
- Is every claim true?

## What to Change and What to Keep

Not everything on your resume should change. This table shows what to edit for each job.

| Resume section | Change for each job? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Contact details | No | Keep them the same. |
| Summary | Yes | Rewrite for the role and its top needs. |
| Job titles | Usually no | Keep your real title. You can add a short note, such as "Office Manager (operations and purchasing)", if your title hides what you did. |
| Bullets | Yes | Reorder, rewrite with the employer's words, cut unrelated ones. |
| Skills | Yes | Match the order and wording of the post. |
| Education | Rarely | Add relevant coursework only for early-career roles. |
| Dates and employers | Never | These must stay accurate. |

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Keyword stuffing. Do not repeat a term ten times or hide keywords in white text. This makes your resume hard to read and looks dishonest.
- Changing job titles to match the post. A background check can show your real title. Use a note in parentheses instead.
- Tailoring only the skills section. The bullets are where you prove the skills.
- Forgetting the file name. Save each version as "[Your Name] Resume [Company].pdf" so you send the right one.
- Using a design with tables, columns, or text in images. Some systems read these badly. A simple one-column layout is the safest choice for most online applications.

## Tailor Your Cover Letter at the Same Time

You already have the target terms and your best matching results. Use the same two or three results in your cover letter, and explain why they matter for this job. Our guide on [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter) shows how to turn those results into a short, direct letter.

## Quick Checklist

- Master resume saved and up to date
- 8 to 12 target terms pulled from the post
- Summary rewritten for this role
- Top bullets in each job match the target terms
- Skills listed in the order of the job post
- Every claim is true
- File named for this company
- Resume saved in a folder with the job post, so you can read both before an interview

After a few applications, this process becomes fast. You will reuse the same rewritten bullets for similar jobs, and you will only need small changes for each new post.
