To message a recruiter on LinkedIn, send a short, specific note. Say who you are, name the exact role, give one proof that you fit, and ask a clear question. Keep it to four or five sentences. Apply for the job first, then send the message, so the recruiter can find your application. A short note that names the role gets more replies than a long note about your whole career.

## Why a Short Message Works Better

Recruiters receive many messages each day. They read on a phone between calls. A long message asks them to do work: find the role, find your fit, and guess what you want. A short message does that work for them.

Your message has one job. It must make the recruiter open your profile or your application. It does not need to tell your full story. Your profile and your resume do that.

## Before You Send the Message

Do these steps first. They take about 15 minutes and they make your message much stronger.

1. Find the correct recruiter. Look at the job post for a name. If there is no name, search LinkedIn for "[Company] recruiter" or "[Company] talent acquisition" and look for someone who hires for your type of role.
2. Apply for the job through the company site or the job board.
3. Update your LinkedIn profile. The recruiter clicks your name first. Make sure that your headline says what you do. See these [LinkedIn headline examples](/blog/linkedin-headline-examples) if your headline is weak.
4. Pick one proof point. Choose the one result from your experience that matches the job post best.
5. Decide what you ask for. Do you want a call, a referral to the hiring manager, or only a confirmation that they saw your application?

## How to Message a Recruiter on LinkedIn: The Structure

Every good recruiter message has the same parts. Use them in this order.

1. A greeting with the recruiter's first name.
2. The role, with the job title and, if possible, the job ID.
3. Your fit in one sentence, with one result or skill.
4. The fact that you applied (and when).
5. One clear, easy question.
6. A thank you and your name.

Do not open with "I hope this message finds you well." It uses space and says nothing. Start with the role.

## Template 1: Connection Request Note

LinkedIn limits the length of a connection note, so keep this one very short. Use it when you are not yet connected to the recruiter.

> Hi [Recruiter Name], I applied for the [Job Title] role at [Company] today. I have [number] years in [field], most recently [one result]. I would like to connect and learn if my background fits what the team needs. Thanks, [Your Name]

If your note is too long, remove the result first and keep the role and the request.

## Template 2: Message After You Applied

Use this one when you are already connected, or when you send an InMail. InMail usually needs a paid LinkedIn plan.

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I applied for the [Job Title] role ([Job ID]) at [Company] on [date]. The post asks for [key requirement]. In my current role at [Current Company], I [specific result that matches the requirement].
>
> Could you tell me if my background fits what the hiring team wants? I am happy to send more details or set up a short call.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your Name]
> [Link to portfolio, if it helps]

This message works because the recruiter can check your fit in a few seconds. The job ID helps them find the right requisition.

## Template 3: Message Before a Role Is Posted

Sometimes you want to work at a company, but you do not see a matching job. You can still reach out. Ask about the future, not about a job that does not exist.

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> I follow [Company] because of [specific product, project, or team]. I am a [your role] with experience in [skill 1] and [skill 2], and I am looking for my next role.
>
> Do you hire for [type of role] on your team? If yes, I would be glad to send my resume so you have it when a role opens.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your Name]

This message is easy to answer with "yes" or "no". That makes a reply more likely.

## Template 4: Message to a Recruiter Who Contacted You First

When a recruiter messages you, answer within a day or two if you are interested. Make it easy for them to move to the next step.

> Hi [Recruiter Name], thank you for reaching out about the [Job Title] role. I am interested. I am available for a call on [day] or [day] between [time] and [time] [time zone]. My resume is ready if you want it before the call. Best, [Your Name]

If you are not interested, still reply. Send a short note such as "Thank you, this role is not a fit for me now. Please keep me in mind for [type of role]." It keeps the contact useful for later.

## How to Follow Up When the Recruiter Does Not Reply

No reply is normal. Recruiters are busy, and some messages get lost. Wait about one week, then send one follow-up in the same thread.

> Hi [Recruiter Name], I want to follow up on my note about the [Job Title] role. I am still very interested, and I recently [new detail: finished a certification, shipped a project, or similar]. Is there anything more I can send you? Thanks, [Your Name]

Add something new when you can. A follow-up that only says "just checking in" gives the recruiter no reason to answer. If you still get no reply after the follow-up, stop and focus on other roles. For more on timing, read [how to follow up on a job application](/blog/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application).

## Mistakes That Stop a Reply

These are the most common problems in messages to recruiters.

- No role named. "I am looking for opportunities" gives the recruiter nothing to act on.
- A copy of your whole resume in the message. Give one proof point, not ten.
- A demand. "Please call me today" puts pressure on a stranger.
- The wrong recruiter. A recruiter who hires engineers cannot help you with a sales role.
- A generic message sent to many people. Recruiters can tell. Name something specific about the company or the role.
- Spelling errors in the recruiter's name or the company name.
- Many follow-ups. One follow-up is enough.

## Make Your Profile Match Your Message

Your message makes a promise. Your profile must keep it. Your message may say that you are a data analyst with SQL and Tableau experience. Then the recruiter must see that in your headline, your About section, and your recent job.

Check these points before you send:

- Your headline names your role or the role you want.
- Your most recent job lists results, not only duties.
- Your About section uses some of the same words as the job post.
- Your contact settings let recruiters reach you.

If you also send a resume later, tailor it to the job post. This guide shows how to [tailor your resume to a job description](/blog/tailor-resume-to-job-description).

## A Quick Checklist Before You Click Send

Read your message one more time and check each point:

- The recruiter's name is spelled correctly.
- The job title is exact, and the job ID is included if you have it.
- Your one proof point matches a requirement in the job post.
- The message has one question, and the question is easy to answer.
- The message is short enough to read on a phone screen without scrolling much.
- You applied for the job, or you say clearly that no role is posted yet.

When you know how to message a recruiter on LinkedIn, each message takes about five minutes to write. Save your best version as a template, and change the role, the proof point, and the question each time.
