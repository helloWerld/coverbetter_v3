To look for a job while employed, keep the search private and separate from your current work. Use your personal phone, email, and laptop. Schedule interviews outside your core hours or take personal time for them. Do not tell coworkers until you have a signed offer. A quiet, steady search lets you choose your next role without pressure, because you still have income.

## Why Search While You Still Have a Job

A job search while employed has real advantages. You have income, so you can say no to roles that do not fit. You can negotiate from a stronger position, because you do not need the job.

The challenge is time and privacy. You must do the search in a few hours each week, and you must protect your current job while you do it.

## Rules to Keep Your Search Private

Follow these rules from the first day:

1. Use only personal devices. Your employer may be able to monitor company laptops, phones, and networks.
2. Use a personal email address and a personal phone number on your resume.
3. Do not search, apply, or take calls on company time or at your desk.
4. Do not store your resume or cover letters on company systems or cloud drives.
5. Do not tell coworkers, even close ones. News travels.
6. Do not post about your search in public.
7. Keep your current work at the same level. A drop in performance gets attention.

## How to Look for a Job While Employed: Step by Step

### Step 1: Decide What You Want

Write down why you want to leave and what your next job must have. Name the role, the pay range, the location or remote setup, and two or three must-haves. This keeps your search focused and saves time.

### Step 2: Make a Weekly Plan

You have limited hours, so plan them. For example, you might use two weekday evenings and one weekend morning. Set a small weekly goal, such as five strong applications and two networking messages. This guide on how to build a [job search plan](/blog/job-search-plan) shows a full method.

### Step 3: Update Your Materials Quietly

Update your resume and cover letter on your personal computer. Update your LinkedIn profile in small steps over several days. Many sudden changes at once can look like a signal. If LinkedIn lets you turn off notifications to your network about profile changes, turn them off first.

### Step 4: Use Your Network With Care

Referrals are a strong channel when you have little time. Ask people you trust outside your company. This guide on [how to ask for a referral](/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-referral) has message templates. Ask each contact to keep your search confidential.

### Step 5: Track Everything

With a full-time job, it is easy to forget which roles you applied to. Keep a simple [job application tracker](/blog/job-application-tracker) with the company, the role, the date, the contact, and the next step.

### Step 6: Ask Recruiters for Confidentiality

When you talk to a recruiter, say that your search is confidential. This is a normal request.

> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you for your message about the [Job Title] role. I am interested. I am currently employed at [Current Company], so I ask that you keep my application confidential and do not contact my current employer.
>
> I am available for a call on [day] before [time] or after [time] [time zone], or during my lunch break from [time] to [time].
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## How to Schedule Interviews

Interviews are the hardest part of a search while employed. Use these options:

- Ask for early morning, lunchtime, or late afternoon slots.
- Ask if the first rounds can be by phone or video.
- Take a call from your car, a quiet room outside the office, or your home.
- Take a personal day or a half day for a final round that needs many hours.
- Group interviews from different companies on the same day off, if you can.

When you take time off, you do not need to give a detailed reason. "I have a personal appointment" is enough. Do not invent a story, such as a fake illness. A lie is hard to keep up and can hurt your reputation.

For a video interview from home, check the background and the light, and keep your current company's logo off the screen.

## How to Handle References

Employers usually ask for references near the end of the process. Do not list your current manager unless you want them to know.

Use these references instead:

- A former manager from a previous job.
- A former coworker who has left your current company.
- A client or a partner who knows your work, if that is normal in your field.

Tell the hiring company that they may contact your current employer only after you accept an offer. This is a common request.

## What to Say in Interviews About Your Current Job

Interviewers will ask why you want to leave. Keep your answer positive and about the future.

> "I have learned a lot at [Current Company], especially in [skill]. I want a role where I can [goal, such as lead a team], and this role offers that."

Do not criticize your current employer. Do not share confidential information about your current company, its clients, or its plans.

## When You Get an Offer

Do not resign until you have the final offer in writing and you have accepted it. If the offer depends on a background check or references, wait until those are complete if you can.

Then resign in a professional way:

1. Read your contract for your notice period and any terms about leaving.
2. Tell your manager first, in person or by video if you can.
3. Follow with a short written resignation that gives your last day.
4. Offer to help with the handover.
5. Work well until your last day.

Your current employer may make a counteroffer. Think about why you started your search. Often the reasons you wanted to leave do not change with a higher salary.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Applying from your work email or computer.
- Taking interview calls in the open office.
- Leaving a printed resume on the office printer.
- Telling a coworker "in secret".
- Taking many sudden days off in a row.
- Letting your current work slip.
- Resigning before the offer is final and in writing.

Rules about notice periods, contracts, and at-will employment differ by state and by country. Check your contract and local rules before you resign.

When you know how to look for a job while employed, the search becomes a calm, steady process. You protect your income, you keep your reputation, and you choose your next role with care.
