A good LinkedIn headline says what you do, what you work on, and one proof point. It uses plain words that recruiters search for. For a job seeker, the simplest strong form is "[Job Title] | [Top Skill or Specialty] | [Result or Industry]". The LinkedIn headline examples below follow that formula for many roles and situations, so you can copy one and change the details.

Your headline appears under your name on your profile, in search results, in comments, and in connection requests. That makes it one of the most visible lines you write in a job search.

## Why Your Headline Matters

Recruiters search LinkedIn with job titles and skills. Your headline is one of the fields that search reads. If it says only "Seeking new opportunities", a search for "Data Analyst" may not show you near the top.

LinkedIn often fills the headline with your current job title and company by default. That can be fine. But if your current title is unclear, or if you want a different role, write your own.

## A Simple Formula for Your Headline

Use this structure and change it as you need:

> [Job Title you want or have] | [Specialty or Top Skills] | [Proof point, industry, or audience]

Some tips for each part:

- Job title: use the common title that appears in job posts, not an internal title. "Customer Success Manager" is better than "Client Happiness Hero".
- Specialty or skills: name two or three skills or tools from the job posts you want.
- Proof point: a short result, a certification, or the type of company you know best.

Use a vertical bar (|), a comma, or a bullet to separate the parts. Keep it easy to read.

## LinkedIn Headline Examples for Job Seekers

If you are between jobs, you can still lead with your title. A recruiter searches for the role, not for "unemployed".

- Marketing Coordinator | Email, Social, Events | Open to Full-Time Roles in Austin
- Senior Product Designer | Design Systems, Research | Seeking Remote Product Roles
- Supply Chain Analyst | Forecasting, ERP | Available Immediately
- Paralegal | Litigation Support, E-Discovery | Looking for Roles in Chicago

Add your location or "Remote" only if it helps your search. Use the Open to Work setting for the rest.

## Headlines for Career Changers

Name the role you are moving toward, then connect it to your past.

- Aspiring UX Designer | Former Teacher | Google UX Design Certificate
- Data Analyst | Moving From Finance | SQL, Python, Excel
- Project Coordinator | 8 Years in Hospitality Operations | CAPM
- Customer Success | Former Nurse Who Explains Complex Things Simply

"Aspiring" is fine when you do not yet have experience in the new title. If you have done the work in projects or freelance jobs, use the title without "aspiring".

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Only words like "Passionate", "Motivated", or "Hardworking". They do not say what you do.
- A clever title that no one searches for, such as "Spreadsheet Wizard".
- A long list of every skill you have. Pick the ones that match your target jobs.
- A headline that does not match your resume or your experience section.
- All capital letters.
- Emojis in a conservative industry. In some creative fields they are fine, but they add no search value.

## How to Pick the Right Keywords

Open five job posts for the role you want. Note the job title they use and the skills that appear in most of them. Use those words in your headline, in your About section, and in your experience bullets. The method is the same one you use for your resume, and the guide on [resume keywords](/blog/resume-keywords) walks through it.

Your headline is one part of a larger plan. If you are starting a search now, the guide on how to build a [job search plan](/blog/job-search-plan) shows where LinkedIn fits each week.

## How to Change Your Headline

LinkedIn changes its layout from time to time, but the steps are usually these:

1. Go to your profile.
2. Select the pencil icon in the top section, next to your name.
3. Find the Headline field.
4. Type your new headline and check the character count.
5. Save.

LinkedIn may ask whether to share the profile change with your network. If you are job searching while employed, you can turn that off.

## Check Your Headline

Read your headline as a recruiter would. Ask:

- Does it include the job title I want?
- Does it name skills from real job posts?
- Is there one proof point, such as a certification, an industry, or a result?
- Does the start of the line make sense alone, in case LinkedIn cuts it short?

If the answer is yes to each, your headline does its job.
