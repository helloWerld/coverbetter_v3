To end a cover letter, write a short final paragraph that restates your fit in one sentence, asks for an interview, and thanks the reader. Then add a professional sign-off, such as "Sincerely" or "Best regards", and your full name. Keep the whole close to three or four sentences, and make the call to action clear.

The end of the letter is the part the hiring manager reads last, so it shapes what they remember. This guide gives you a structure, example closing lines, sign-offs to use and avoid, and a full template.

## The Three Parts of a Strong Close

A good cover letter ending has three parts. Each is one or two sentences.

1. A summary of your value. Connect your strongest skill to the main need of the job.
2. A call to action. Say that you want to talk, and make it easy to reply.
3. A thank-you. Thank the reader for their time.

After that comes the sign-off, then your name and contact details. The guide on [cover letter format](/blog/cover-letter-format) shows where each part goes on the page.

## How to End a Cover Letter: Step by Step

### Step 1: Restate your fit in one sentence

Do not repeat your whole letter. Pick the one skill or result that matters most for this job.

Weak: "I believe I would be a great fit for this role."

Better: "My five years running payroll for multi-state teams match the growth plans you described for [Company]."

The better line names a real skill and a real need. The reader can see the link without guessing.

### Step 2: Ask for the next step

Say what you want: a conversation or an interview. You do not need to be pushy. You do need to be direct.

- "I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help your team reach [goal]."
- "Could we set up a call to talk about the [Job Title] role?"
- "I am available for an interview at your convenience and can start [timeframe]."

### Step 3: Thank the reader

One short sentence is enough. "Thank you for your time and consideration." works well. You can also make it specific: "Thank you for reading my application."

### Step 4: Choose a sign-off

Pick a sign-off that fits the industry and the tone of your letter. Put a comma after it, then your full name on the next line.

### Step 5: Add your contact details

If your contact details are not already in the header, add your phone number and email under your name. Add a LinkedIn or portfolio link if it helps the reader.

## Example Closing Paragraphs

Change the bracketed parts so each line is about the real job.

Confident tone:

> I have built the kind of [skill or system] that [Company] needs to [goal from the job post]. I would like to talk about how I can do the same for your team. Thank you for your time and consideration.

Friendly tone:

> I would love to bring my experience in [skill] to the team at [Company], and I would be glad to share more in a conversation. Thank you for reading my application.

Career change:

> My background in [old field] gives me a different view of [new field problem], and I am ready to apply it to the [Job Title] role. I would welcome the chance to explain how my skills transfer. Thank you for your consideration.

Entry level:

> I am eager to learn from your team and contribute to [specific project or goal] from my first week. I would be grateful for the chance to interview. Thank you for your time.

Referral:

> [Referrer Name] said that your team values [quality], and I would be glad to show you how I work. I am available to talk any time next week. Thank you for considering my application.

## Sign-Offs: Which to Use

| Sign-off | When it fits |
| --- | --- |
| Sincerely | Formal letters, conservative industries, any time you are unsure |
| Best regards | Most professional settings, email and letter |
| Kind regards | Professional and a little warmer; common in many countries |
| Respectfully | Government, military, and very formal settings |
| Thank you | Works well when your last sentence is not already a thank-you |
| Best | Email applications in casual industries |

Avoid these in a cover letter:

- "Cheers", "Thanks!", or "Talk soon". They are too casual for most first contacts.
- "Love" or "Warmly". They are too personal.
- "Yours truly". It is not wrong, but it sounds old to many US readers.
- No sign-off at all. The letter looks unfinished.

Conventions differ by country. In the UK, for example, many people use "Yours sincerely" when they know the name and "Yours faithfully" when they do not.

## Full Cover Letter Ending Template

Copy this block and replace the brackets.

> [One sentence that connects your top skill or result to the company's main need.] I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help [Company] [goal from the job post]. Thank you for your time and consideration.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> [Your Full Name]
> [Phone Number] | [Email Address]
> [LinkedIn or Portfolio URL]

## Mistakes to Avoid at the End

- Ending with a question about pay, benefits, or vacation.
- Writing "I will call you next week to set up an interview." It can sound like pressure.
- Apologizing for gaps or lack of experience in the last line. Address a gap earlier, once, and end on a strength.
- Using "Hope to hear from you soon!" with an exclamation mark.
- Repeating the first paragraph word for word.
- Leaving a name or job title from another letter in the close. Check this every time.

## Make the Ending Match the Opening

The best letters feel like one argument from start to end. If your first line is about a specific result, your last line can return to it. For example, open with how you cut onboarding time at your last job, and close by offering to do the same at the new company. The guide on [cover letter opening lines](/blog/cover-letter-opening-lines) has openers that pair well with the closes above.

Keep the length in check too. A close of three or four sentences fits the usual one-page letter. If your letter runs long, cut from the middle, not from the close. The guide on [how long a cover letter should be](/blog/how-long-should-a-cover-letter-be) explains the usual limits.

## Final Check

Read the last paragraph alone. Ask yourself three questions:

- Does it name a real skill and a real need of this company?
- Does it ask for a conversation in a clear way?
- Is the sign-off right for this industry, with your full name under it?

If the answer to each is yes, your cover letter ends well.
