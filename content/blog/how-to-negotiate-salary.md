To negotiate salary, wait for an offer and thank the employer. Then ask for a specific higher number, and support it with market research and the value you bring. Say it clearly, once, and then wait for their answer. If base salary cannot move, ask about other parts of the package. Examples are a signing bonus, extra paid time off, or an earlier salary review. Many employers expect a counteroffer, so a polite, specific request is a normal part of the offer stage.

## When to Negotiate

The best time to negotiate is after you receive an offer and before you accept it. At that point, the employer has decided that they want you. They have spent time and money on interviews, and they want to close the process.

Before the offer, you only share your expectations. This guide explains how to answer "[What are your salary expectations?](/blog/salary-expectations-interview-answer)" in the early stages. The real negotiation starts with the offer.

## How to Negotiate Salary in 7 Steps

### Step 1: Get the Full Offer in Writing

Ask for the details of the whole package: base salary, bonus, equity, benefits, paid time off, start date, and any other terms. You cannot judge an offer from the base salary alone.

### Step 2: Thank Them and Ask for Time

You do not need to answer on the spot. Say thank you and ask for a day or two to review.

> "Thank you, I am very happy to receive this offer. Could I have until [day] to review the details? I will come back to you then."

Most employers expect this and agree.

### Step 3: Research Your Number

Compare the offer with the market range for the role, the level, and the location. Use the pay range in the job post, salary data sites, the [US Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/), and people in your network.

Then choose two numbers:

- Your target: the salary you will ask for.
- Your walk-away number: the lowest offer you would accept.

### Step 4: List Your Reasons

A counteroffer is stronger when you give reasons. Good reasons are about value and the market, not about your personal costs.

- The market range for the role is higher than the offer.
- You bring a skill or a certification that the job post asked for.
- Your experience is above the level the role requires.
- You have another offer or a strong current position.

### Step 5: Make the Counteroffer

Ask for one specific number. A specific figure, such as $92,000, sounds more researched than a round range. Say it once and then stop talking. The silence after your number is normal. Do not fill it with "but I am flexible".

### Step 6: Listen and Respond

The employer may say yes, offer a number between theirs and yours, or say no. Each answer gives you information.

- If they say yes, thank them and ask for the updated offer in writing.
- If they meet you partway, decide if the new number is above your walk-away number.
- If they say no to more base salary, ask about other parts of the package.

### Step 7: Get the Final Offer in Writing

Do not resign from your current job or decline other offers until you have the final, signed offer in writing.

## Scripts for Each Situation

### Phone Script for a Counteroffer

> "Thank you again for the offer. I am excited about the role and the team. Based on my research for [Job Title] roles in [location], and my experience with [key skill], I was hoping for a base salary of [$X]. Is there flexibility to get closer to that number?"

### When You Have Another Offer

Use this only if it is true.

> "[Company] is my first choice. I have another offer at [$X] base salary. If you can match it, I am ready to accept."

### When Base Salary Is Fixed

> "I understand that the base salary is set. Could we look at other parts of the package? A signing bonus of [$X] or [number] extra days of paid time off would help close the gap."

### When the Offer Is Already Good

You can still ask, even when you like the offer.

> "This is a strong offer, and I am ready to say yes. Is there any room for a small increase in base salary, or for a signing bonus?"

## Email Template for a Salary Counteroffer

> Subject: [Your Name], offer for [Job Title]
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you again for the offer for the [Job Title] role. I am excited about the chance to join [Company] and to work on [team goal or project].
>
> I reviewed the details and the market range for this role in [location]. With my [number] years of experience in [key skill], I would like to ask for a base salary of [$X].
>
> I am confident that I can deliver [specific value, for example a result from your past work that matches the role]. If we can agree on this number, I am ready to accept.
>
> Thank you for considering it. I am happy to talk by phone if that is easier.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## What Else You Can Negotiate

| Item | When it helps most |
|---|---|
| Signing bonus | The base salary is fixed, or you lose a bonus at your current job |
| Paid time off | You already have more time off at your current employer |
| Start date | You need time to give notice or to rest between jobs |
| Remote or hybrid days | Your commute or your family situation needs flexibility |
| Title | The title affects your next job search |
| Salary review date | The company wants to see results before a larger raise |
| Training budget | You want a certification or a course that helps the role |
| Equity | The company is private and equity is part of total pay |

Choose the two or three items that matter most to you. A long list of requests can make the conversation harder.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Accepting on the spot when you wanted to negotiate.
- Giving a range when the employer asks for your counteroffer. They will hear the low end.
- Making personal reasons the main argument, such as rent or debt.
- Lying about another offer.
- Making many rounds of requests. Ask for everything that matters in one conversation.
- Negotiating with an angry or demanding tone.
- Resigning from your current job before you have the final offer in writing.

## Know When to Stop

Negotiation has a natural end. When the employer says that this is their best and final offer, decide: accept, or decline politely. Do not keep pushing after that point.

Compare the final offer with your walk-away number, not with your target. If it is above your walk-away number and the role fits your goals, it is often a good offer to accept.

## A Note on Contracts and Rules

Some offers include terms such as non-compete clauses, notice periods, or bonus repayment rules. These terms can matter as much as salary. Read the contract carefully, and check local rules or ask a qualified adviser if a term is unclear. Rules differ by state and country.

Learning how to negotiate salary takes one or two practice runs. Write your script, say it aloud to a friend, and use it on your next offer.
