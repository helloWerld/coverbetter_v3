Resume action verbs are strong, specific verbs that start your resume bullets and say exactly what you did, such as "built", "negotiated", "reduced", or "trained". They replace weak phrases like "responsible for" and "helped with". Pick the verb that matches the real work, then follow it with the task and the result.

This page gives you resume action verbs grouped by skill, a simple formula for each bullet, and before-and-after examples.

## Why Action Verbs Matter

A hiring manager reads the start of each bullet first. A weak start, such as "Responsible for", tells them about your duties but not your work. A strong start, such as "Cut", "Launched", or "Rebuilt", tells them what changed because you were there.

Strong verbs also make your resume shorter. "Was responsible for the training of new employees" becomes "Trained new employees". The second version says the same thing in a third of the words.

## The Bullet Formula

Use this structure for most bullets:

> [Action verb] + [what you did] + [how or with what] + [result]

Examples:

- "Reduced invoice errors by 30% by building a checklist for the month-end close."
- "Trained 12 new support agents on Zendesk and our refund policy."
- "Launched a weekly email newsletter that grew to 4,000 subscribers in a year."

Use a number only if it is true and you can explain it in an interview. If you do not have a number, describe the scope: how many people, how often, how large, or for whom.

## Weak Phrases and What to Use Instead

| Weak phrase | Stronger choice |
| --- | --- |
| Responsible for managing | Managed |
| Helped with | Supported, assisted, or the exact task |
| Worked on | Built, wrote, tested, or the exact task |
| In charge of | Led, directed, oversaw |
| Duties included | Start with the verb for the main duty |
| Participated in | Contributed to, or the exact part you did |
| Was tasked with | Start with the verb for the task |
| Utilized | Used |

"Helped" is not always wrong. If you truly played a supporting role, say so. But then add what you did: "Helped plan the move to a new CRM by cleaning 8,000 contact records."

## Before and After Examples

Before: "Responsible for social media accounts."
After: "Grew the company Instagram account from 2,000 to 9,500 followers in 10 months by posting short how-to videos."

Before: "Helped with customer complaints."
After: "Resolved about 40 customer complaints a week by phone and chat, with refunds or replacements within policy."

Before: "Worked on the new website."
After: "Rewrote 25 product pages for the new website and added clear size charts."

Before: "In charge of inventory."
After: "Tracked inventory for a 3,000-item warehouse and cut stock-count errors by setting up weekly cycle counts."

Each "after" line starts with a verb, names the task, and gives a scope or a result. Use these as a pattern, and put in your own true details.

## How to Choose the Right Verb

- Be exact. "Wrote" is better than "created" when you wrote something.
- Match the level of your role. "Led" means you were in charge. "Coordinated" means you organized the work of others. Use the one that is true.
- Mirror the job post. If the post says "manage vendor relationships", "Managed relationships with 15 vendors" is a direct match.
- Avoid buzzwords that say nothing, such as "spearheaded synergies".
- Vary your verbs, but do not reach for strange words just to avoid repetition.

## Action Verbs and Keywords Together

Action verbs say what you did. Keywords say with what. A strong bullet uses both. For example: "Analyzed monthly sales data in SQL and Tableau to find the 10 slowest-moving products." Here "Analyzed" is the verb, and "SQL" and "Tableau" are the keywords.

## Action Verbs Outside Your Resume

The same verbs work in your cover letter and on LinkedIn. In a cover letter, use them in full sentences. For example: "I rebuilt our onboarding guide for new hires." On LinkedIn, use them in the bullets of your experience section, the same way as on your resume.

In an interview, verbs help too. When you tell a story, say "I" and a clear verb, such as "I called the client" or "I rewrote the plan". This shows your own part in the work.

## A Quick Checklist for Each Bullet

- Starts with an action verb in the right tense.
- Names a specific task, not a general duty.
- Includes a tool, a skill, or a scope where it helps.
- Ends with a result or a reason when you have one.
- Fits on one or two lines.
- Is true, and you can explain it in an interview.

Go through your resume one job at a time. Fix the first word of each bullet first. That single change often makes the whole page easier to read.
