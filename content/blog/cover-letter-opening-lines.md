Good cover letter opening lines name the job you want and give the reader one specific reason to keep reading, in one or two sentences. The strongest openings start with a relevant result, a real connection to the company, or a referral. Avoid "I am writing to apply for..." on its own, because it tells the reader nothing new. Write a new opening for each job, because it is the part the reader is most likely to read.

## Why the Opening Matters

A hiring manager often reads many letters for one role. The first lines decide how carefully they read the rest. An opening that sounds like every other letter gives them a reason to skim. An opening with a real detail gives them a reason to slow down.

The opening also sets your tone. A clear, specific first sentence shows that you can write clearly at work.

## A Formula for Cover Letter Opening Lines

Use this formula for almost any job:

> [Hook: a result, a connection, or a referral] + [the exact job title and company] + [why you fit, in a few words]

Fill-in version:

> [Result or connection that matches the job]. That is why I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company], where I can [what you would do for them].

You can change the order. Some people prefer to name the role first, then add the hook. Both work if the hook is specific.

You may start from a draft, for example one that [CoverBetter](/) writes from your resume and the job post. Rewrite its opening line with your own strongest result. That line deserves the most care.

## 25 Cover Letter Opening Lines by Situation

Each example below uses square brackets for details that you replace. Do not copy any line exactly. Use the structure and add your own facts.

### Start with a result

1. "Last year I cut our customer response time from two days to four hours. I want to bring that focus on speed to the Support Lead role at [Company]."
2. "In three years as a sales rep, I grew my territory from [number] to [number] accounts. I am applying for the Account Executive role at [Company] to do the same for your [region] team."
3. "I built the scheduling system that my clinic still uses for 30 staff. I would like to do that kind of work as an Operations Coordinator at [Company]."
4. "My last campaign brought [number] new sign-ups in one month with a budget of [amount]. I am applying for the Growth Marketing role at [Company] because your post asks for exactly this kind of work."
5. "I have closed every month-end on time for [number] years in a row. I am applying for the Senior Accountant role at [Company]."

### Start with a connection to the company

6. "I have used [Product] every day for two years to plan my team's work, and I want to help build it as a Product Designer."
7. "Your team's recent launch of [project] solves a problem I have worked on for years: [problem]. I am applying for the [Job Title] role to help with the next phase."
8. "I first heard about [Company] when your team [specific public action]. Since then I have followed your work, and the [Job Title] role is the first one that matches my skills."
9. "As a long-time customer of [Company], I know how much your support team affects the experience. I am applying for the Customer Success Manager role."

### Start with a referral

10. "[Referrer Name] on your [Team Name] team suggested that I apply for the [Job Title] role. We worked together at [Company], where I [short result]."
11. "When I told [Referrer Name] about my work in [area], she said your team needed someone with that background. I am applying for the [Job Title] role."
12. "I met [Name] at [event], and our conversation about [topic] led me to this [Job Title] opening."

### Start with your expertise

13. "I am a registered nurse with six years in emergency care, and I am applying for the Charge Nurse role at [Hospital]."
14. "I am a data analyst who turns messy sales data into weekly reports that managers use. I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company]."
15. "For [number] years I have managed warehouse teams of up to [number] people. I am applying for the Warehouse Manager role at [Company]."
16. "I write technical documentation that engineers actually read. That is the skill your Technical Writer post asks for."

### Career change

17. "After eight years of teaching, I am moving into corporate training, and the Learning Specialist role at [Company] fits the skills I use every day."
18. "I have spent five years in hospitality solving guest problems in real time. I am now applying those skills to the Customer Success role at [Company]."
19. "My background in nursing gives me a patient's view of health software. That is why I am applying for the Clinical Product Specialist role at [Company]."

### Entry-level or recent graduate

20. "I just finished my degree in [field], where my final project was [short description]. I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company] to use those skills full time."
21. "During my internship at [Company], I [short result]. I want to continue that work as a [Job Title] on your team."
22. "I have worked part time in retail through college, and I learned how to handle busy days and difficult customers. I am applying for the Store Supervisor role."

### Returning to work or internal move

23. "After two years away from work for family reasons, I am ready to return to project management. The [Job Title] role at [Company] matches my experience."
24. "I have been on the [Team Name] team for three years. I am applying for the [Job Title] role so I can help the team in a bigger way."
25. "I am applying for the [Job Title] role because it combines the two parts of my job I enjoy most: [task 1] and [task 2]."

## A Full Opening Paragraph Example

The opening line is one or two sentences. The full opening paragraph adds one more sentence that leads into your proof.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Last year I cut our customer response time from two days to four hours by changing how our team sorted incoming tickets. I am applying for the Support Lead role at [Company] because your post asks for someone who can build simple, fast support processes. I would bring the same approach to your growing team.

If you are not sure what goes above that paragraph, read [how to address a cover letter](/blog/how-to-address-a-cover-letter).

## Openings to Avoid

| Weak opening | Why it is weak | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| "To Whom It May Concern, I am writing to apply for..." | Distant greeting and no new information | Use a name, then start with a result |
| "My name is [Name] and I am applying for..." | Your name is already at the top | Start with the job or a result |
| "I am the perfect candidate for this job." | A claim with no proof | Give the proof and let them decide |
| "I have always dreamed of working at [Company]." | Hard to believe without a reason | Name the real reason you like the company |
| "Are you looking for a passionate team player?" | Sounds like an advertisement | Make a direct statement |
| "Although I do not have all the qualifications..." | Starts with a weakness | Start with what you can do |
| "I saw your job post on [job board]." | Only useful if a person referred you | Name the role and give a hook |

## How to Test Your Opening

Read your cover letter opening lines and ask:

- Does it name the job title?
- Does it include one specific fact: a number, a project, a product, or a name?
- Could someone else send the same sentence to another company? If yes, rewrite it.
- Does it connect to a need in the job post?
- Is it short enough to read in one breath?
