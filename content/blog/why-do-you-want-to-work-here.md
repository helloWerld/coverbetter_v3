To answer "Why do you want to work here?", give one specific reason about the company. Then connect it to your skills, and say how the role fits your goals. For example: "I want to work here because your team builds [product] for [customer group]. I have spent four years on that same problem at [Company]. This role lets me go deeper into [skill], which is where I want to grow." A specific answer shows that you did research and that you chose this company on purpose.

## What the Interviewer Wants to Learn

The question looks simple, but it tests several things at once:

- Did you research the company, or did you apply without reading?
- Do you understand what the company does and who it serves?
- Do your goals match what the role offers, so you will stay?
- Will you bring energy to the work, or is this only a paycheck?

A general answer such as "It is a great company with a great culture" fails every one of these tests. A specific answer passes them.

## Research to Do Before the Interview

You need two or three real details about the company. Spend 20 to 30 minutes on these sources:

1. The company website, especially the "About", "Products", and "Careers" pages.
2. The job post. Read it again and mark what the team wants to achieve.
3. A recent news item, press release, or company blog post.
4. The LinkedIn pages of the company and of the people who will interview you.
5. The product itself, if you can use it or see it.
6. Reviews from customers, if the company sells to the public.

Write down what interests you. Look for a product, a customer group, a mission, a way of working, or a recent change. Choose the detail that connects best to your own experience.

## The 3-Part Formula to Answer "Why Do You Want to Work Here?"

Use this structure for your answer:

1. The company: one specific thing that draws you to them.
2. The fit: how your skills or experience match that thing.
3. The future: what you want to learn or achieve in this role.

Here is the formula as a fill-in template:

> "I want to work at [Company] because [specific detail about the company, product, mission, or team]. In my [number] years as a [role], I have [relevant experience or result], so I can help with [goal from the job post]. This role also lets me [grow in a skill or reach a goal], which is the next step I want in my career."

Each part is one or two sentences. The whole answer takes about one minute.

## Sample Answers

Use these as models, and change every detail to your real facts.

### Software Engineer at a Product Company

> "I want to work here because your app solves a real problem for small business owners. I use it for my own side business, so I know the product from the customer side. In my current role, I build payment features in React and Node, and your post says the team is expanding payments. I want to work on a product I believe in, and I want to grow toward technical leadership on a team like this one."

### Nurse at a Hospital

> "I want to work at [Hospital] because of your focus on patient education after discharge. I have seen how much better patients do when they understand their care plan. In my six years in cardiac care, I created teaching materials for patients with heart failure. I want to bring that experience to a team that shares this focus."

### Marketing Coordinator at a Nonprofit

> "I have followed [Organization]'s work on [cause] since I volunteered at your [event] in [year]. I want my daily work to support a mission like this one. In my current role, I run email campaigns and social media for a retail brand, and I can bring that skill to your donor campaigns. I also want to learn how nonprofit fundraising works from the inside."

### Entry-Level Candidate

> "I want to start my career at [Company] because of your training program for new analysts, which I read about on your careers page. I want to learn from experienced people in a structured way. In my final year, I completed a project on [topic] using Excel and SQL, and I enjoyed turning data into clear recommendations. This role is the work I want to do."

## Mistakes to Avoid

- A general compliment. "You are a leader in the industry" could describe many companies.
- Only what you get. "The benefits are great and the office is close to my home" says nothing about what you give.
- Facts copied from the homepage without any personal link. Connect the fact to you.
- Wrong or old facts. Check that the product or the news you mention is current.
- Too much praise. One clear reason sounds more real than five big compliments.
- A rehearsed speech that sounds memorized. Know the three parts, but say them in your own natural words.

## How This Answer Connects to Other Questions

Your answer here should match what you say in other parts of the interview. Your answer to "[Tell me about yourself](/blog/tell-me-about-yourself)" sets up your background. This answer explains why that background points to this company. Your answer to "Where do you see yourself in five years?" should fit the future part of this answer.

Prepare these answers together, along with other [common interview questions](/blog/common-interview-questions). When the answers fit together, you sound focused and honest.

## Use the Same Research at the End of the Interview

The details you find also help you at the end of the interview, when the interviewer asks if you have questions. Turn one detail into a question. For example: "I read that you launched [product] last quarter. How does this role support that work?" This guide to [questions to ask in an interview](/blog/questions-to-ask-in-an-interview) has more ideas.

## Practice Checklist

- You have one specific reason about the company, based on research.
- You connect that reason to a skill or a result of your own.
- You say what you want to learn or achieve in this role.
- Your answer takes about one minute.
- Your facts are current and correct.
- You practiced the answer aloud at least three times.

The interviewer asks "Why do you want to work here?" to learn if you chose them on purpose. A short answer with one true detail, one link to your skills, and one goal gives them that proof.
