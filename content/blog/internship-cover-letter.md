An internship cover letter is a one-page letter that tells an employer two things: why you want this internship, and why you can do the work. Use your classes, projects, part-time jobs, clubs, and volunteer work as proof. Keep it to about 250 to 350 words, name the internship and the term, and match two or three skills to the posting.

Most internship applicants have the same problem: the resume looks thin. The cover letter is where you fix that. It gives you room to explain one project in detail. It also shows that you read the posting and that you will be easy to work with.

## What an Internship Cover Letter Must Do

A hiring manager who reads an internship cover letter does not expect years of results. They want answers to a few simple questions:

- Do you know what this internship is and what the team does?
- Can you do the basic tasks in the posting, or learn them fast?
- Did you finish things before, such as a project, a job, or a club role?
- Are you available for the dates and hours of the program?
- Will you be reliable and easy to work with?

Every paragraph in your letter should answer one or more of these questions. If a sentence does not, cut it.

## How to Write an Internship Cover Letter in 5 Parts

### 1. The header and greeting

Put your name, email, phone number, and city at the top. Add a LinkedIn or portfolio link if it shows relevant work. Then address a real person if you can find one. Check the posting, the company careers page, and LinkedIn for the internship coordinator or team lead. If you cannot find a name, "Dear [Team Name] Hiring Team" is fine. For more options, read [how to address a cover letter](/blog/how-to-address-a-cover-letter).

### 2. The opening paragraph

Name the internship, the term, and your school and year. Then give one specific reason you want this internship at this company. A specific reason is a product you use, a project the team published, or a skill the posting names that matches your studies.

Example opening lines:

- "I am a junior in marketing at [University], and I am applying for the [Season Year] Marketing Internship at [Company]."
- "Your posting asks for someone who can clean and chart data in Excel. I did that every week this year for my research lab."

### 3. The proof paragraph

This is the most important part. Pick one or two experiences and describe them with facts: what you did, what tools you used, and what happened. Class projects count. A part-time job counts. A club role counts.

Weak: "I am a hard worker with strong communication skills."

Strong: "As treasurer of the [Club Name], I tracked a budget of [amount] in Google Sheets and sent a monthly report to 40 members."

Use numbers only when they are true. A small, real number is better than a big, vague claim.

### 4. The fit paragraph

Connect your skills to two or three requirements in the posting. Use the same words that the posting uses, if they are true for you. This also helps if the company uses an applicant tracking system, because many of these systems search for terms from the posting. Then say what you want to learn. Internships are training programs, and a clear learning goal shows that you will use the time well.

### 5. The close

Confirm your availability (dates, hours, and whether you can work on site, hybrid, or remote). Thank the reader and ask for a conversation. Sign with your full name.

## Internship Cover Letter Template

Copy this template and replace every item in square brackets.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I am a [year] studying [major] at [University], and I am applying for the [Internship Title] for [Season Year] at [Company]. I want to join [Company] because [specific reason: a product, a project, or a team goal you read about].
>
> In [class, job, or club], I [specific task] using [tool or skill]. The result was [what happened, with a true number if you have one]. That work taught me [skill from the posting], which your posting lists as a key part of the role.
>
> Your posting also asks for [requirement 2] and [requirement 3]. I [one line of proof for requirement 2], and I [one line of proof for requirement 3]. During the internship, I want to learn [specific skill or area], and I am ready to take on the routine tasks that help the team.
>
> I am available from [start date] to [end date] for [hours per week], and I can work [on site / hybrid / remote]. Thank you for your time. I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help [Team Name] this [season].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]

## Full Internship Cover Letter Example

Here is a complete example for a data analyst internship. The student and the company are fictional.

> Dear Ms. Rivera,
>
> I am a junior studying economics at Lakeview State University, and I am applying for the Summer 2028 Data Analyst Internship at Northpoint Foods. I use your store app every week, and I want to help the team that decides which offers customers see in it.
>
> This year, I worked as a research assistant in the economics department. I cleaned survey data from 600 students in Excel, built pivot tables, and made charts for a faculty report. I learned to check every column for errors before I start the analysis, and I learned to explain a chart in two sentences.
>
> Your posting asks for SQL and clear written communication. I completed an introductory SQL course this spring and used it in a class project to compare prices across 12 grocery chains. I also write a weekly summary for my lab, so I am used to short, clear reports. During the internship, I want to learn how a retail team tests offers and measures the results.
>
> I am available from June 1 to August 14, full time, and I can work on site in Chicago. Thank you for your time. I would welcome a conversation about how I can help your analytics team this summer.
>
> Sincerely,
> Jordan Lee

## What to Write When You Have Little Experience

If you have no job and no big project yet, use what you have. These sources all count as proof:

| Source | What to show | Example line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class project | A skill from the posting and a finished result | "I built a budget model in Excel for a team of four." |
| Part-time job | Reliability, customer contact, and responsibility | "I trained two new staff members on the register." |
| Club or team | Organization and teamwork | "I planned three events and managed the sign-up list." |
| Volunteer work | Commitment and people skills | "I tutored two middle school students every week for a year." |
| Personal project | Initiative and a tool you learned alone | "I built a small website to track my running times." |

For more ideas, read our guide to a [cover letter with no experience](/blog/cover-letter-with-no-experience).

## Common Internship Cover Letter Mistakes

- You write about what the internship will do for you, and not about what you can do for the team.
- You copy your resume line by line. The letter should add detail, not repeat.
- You use the wrong company name. This happens when you reuse a letter. Read every name twice.
- You write more than one page. Most internship letters need three or four short paragraphs. See [how long a cover letter should be](/blog/how-long-should-a-cover-letter-be) for word counts.
- You forget your availability. Programs have fixed dates, so state yours.
- You use general praise such as "your company is a leader in the industry." Give a specific reason instead.

## Final Checklist Before You Send

- The letter names the internship title, the term, and your school.
- The opening gives one specific reason for this company.
- One paragraph describes a real project or job with facts.
- You match two or three requirements from the posting, in the posting's words.
- You state your dates, hours, and work location.
- The letter is one page and between 250 and 350 words.
- You saved the file as a PDF with your name in the file name, unless the posting asks for another format.
- You read the letter out loud once to find errors.
