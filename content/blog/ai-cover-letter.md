An AI cover letter is a letter that an AI tool drafts from your resume and the job post. You then edit and check it. Used well, AI saves you the hardest part, which is the blank page. Used badly, it produces a generic letter that any applicant could send. The rule is simple: give the AI specific input, then edit the output until every line is true, specific, and in your voice.

## What AI Does Well and What It Does Badly

AI is fast at structure. It can match your experience to the requirements in a job post and put the result in a clean, three-part letter. It rarely makes grammar errors.

AI also has clear weak points:

- It does not know facts that are not in your input. It may fill gaps with general claims.
- It may invent details, such as a number or a project, if your input is thin.
- It likes common phrases, such as "I am excited to apply" and "proven track record".
- It does not know why you want this job. Only you know that.

Your job is to fix these weak points. That takes 10 to 20 minutes per letter, and it is the difference between a letter that works and one that does not.

## How to Write an AI Cover Letter in 6 Steps

### Step 1: Give the AI Your Real Resume

Use your full, current resume, not a short list of skills. The more real results the AI can read, the fewer general claims it makes. If you have results that are not on your resume, add them to your input.

### Step 2: Give It the Full Job Post

Paste the whole job post, including the "About us" part and the list of requirements. The AI uses the employer's words to choose which parts of your experience to put first.

### Step 3: Add One Personal Reason

Write one or two sentences about why you want this job at this company. Name a product, a mission, a team, or a problem that interests you. This is the part that AI cannot create from your resume.

### Step 4: Choose a Tone and a Length

Pick the tone that fits the company. A bank or a law firm usually expects a professional tone. A startup may welcome a friendlier one. Most letters work best at about 250 to 400 words. This guide on [how long a cover letter should be](/blog/how-long-should-a-cover-letter-be) explains the options.

### Step 5: Edit the Draft

Read the draft line by line. Do these edits every time:

1. Check every fact. Delete any number, project, or skill that is not true.
2. Replace general claims with a specific example from your work.
3. Rewrite the first line so it names the role and gives a reason to read on. See these [cover letter opening lines](/blog/cover-letter-opening-lines) for models.
4. Remove common AI phrases (see the list below).
5. Read the letter aloud. Change any sentence that you would never say.

### Step 6: Check the Details

Check the company name, the job title, and the hiring manager's name. Check that the letter does not mention a different company. This error happens when you reuse a draft.

## Example: An AI Draft Before and After Editing

Here is a typical first paragraph from an AI tool with thin input:

> I am excited to apply for the Marketing Coordinator position at [Company]. With my proven track record of success and passion for marketing, I am confident that I would be a valuable addition to your dynamic team.

Every sentence in it could belong to anyone. Here is the same paragraph after the writer added specific input and edited it:

> I am applying for the Marketing Coordinator role at [Company] because your [specific campaign or product] is the kind of work I want to do every day. In my current role at [Current Company], I plan and send our weekly email campaign to [number] customers. I also rebuilt our templates, so the team can launch a campaign in [time] instead of [time].

The second version has a reason, a fact, and a result. It also sounds like a person.

## A Full AI Cover Letter Template to Edit

Use this structure to check your AI draft. Replace each item in square brackets with your own facts.

> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> I am applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company]. [One sentence on why this company or role interests you, with a specific detail.]
>
> In my [number] years as a [current or recent role], I have [main result that matches the top requirement in the job post]. At [Company or Project], I [second result, with a number or a clear outcome]. I also bring [skill from the job post], which I used to [short example].
>
> Your post says that the team needs someone who can [requirement in the employer's words]. That is the work I do best, and I would like to do it for [Company].
>
> Thank you for your time. I would welcome the chance to talk about how I can help with [team goal or project].
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number] | [Email]

For more detail on each part, read the full guide on [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter).

## Phrases to Remove From an AI Draft

These phrases appear often in AI drafts. They take up space and say little.

- "I am excited to apply for..."
- "proven track record"
- "dynamic team" or "fast-paced environment"
- "I am confident that I would be a valuable addition"
- "passionate about" with no example
- "In today's world..."
- "I believe my skills and experience make me a perfect fit"

Replace each one with a fact or delete it. If a sentence still works without the phrase, the phrase was not needed.

## Prompt Tips if You Use a General Chat Tool

A general chat tool does not know what a cover letter needs, so tell it.

- Paste your resume and the job post, and label each one.
- Ask for a letter of a fixed length, such as 300 words.
- Tell it to use only facts from your resume and your notes.
- Ask it to write in plain language, with no clichés.
- Ask for two different first paragraphs, then pick the better one.

A cover letter tool does these steps for you. For example, [CoverBetter](/) writes a first draft from your resume and the job post. You pick one of four tones and three lengths, and you make the changes above in its editor.

## Is an AI Cover Letter Honest?

An AI cover letter is honest when every fact in it is true and you stand behind every sentence. The tool is a writing aid, like a template or a friend who reviews your draft. The facts and the final decisions are yours.

Rules differ by employer. Some job posts ask candidates not to use AI, and some roles test your writing directly. Read the application instructions and follow them. In the interview, you must be able to talk about every claim in your letter, so do not send anything that you cannot explain.

## Final Checklist

- Every fact, number, and project in the letter is true.
- The letter names the company, the role, and one specific reason.
- At least two requirements from the job post appear, with an example for each.
- No phrase from the list above is left.
- The letter sounds like you when you read it aloud.
- The names and the company details are correct.
