A thank you email after an interview is a short note that you send within 24 hours to each person who interviewed you. It thanks them for their time, mentions one specific part of the conversation, and repeats in one sentence why you fit the job. Keep it to 75 to 150 words. Send a separate email to each interviewer, and change the details in each one.

## Why a Thank You Email After an Interview Matters

The interview is over, but the decision is not made. A thank you email keeps your name in front of the team while they compare candidates. It also shows that you listened, that you follow through, and that you write clearly. Those are skills almost every job needs.

The email also gives you one more chance to add something. If you forgot to mention a project, or gave a weak answer to one question, you can add one or two sentences about it.

## When to Send It

| Interview type | When to send |
|---|---|
| Phone or video screen | The same day, or the next morning |
| In-person interview | Within 24 hours |
| Panel interview | Within 24 hours, one email to each panel member |
| Interview on a Friday | The same day, so it arrives before the weekend |
| Final round | Within 24 hours, to each person you met |

If you do not have an interviewer's email address, ask the recruiter or the coordinator. You can write: "Could you share the email addresses of the people I met today? I would like to thank them." You can also send all the notes to the recruiter and ask them to pass them on.

## How to Write a Thank You Email After an Interview

Use this order. Each part is one or two sentences.

1. Subject line. Make it easy to find and clear.
2. Greeting. Use the name the interviewer used with you. If they said "Call me Sam", write "Hi Sam".
3. Thanks. Thank them for their time and name the role.
4. Specific detail. Mention one thing you talked about: a project, a problem, a question they asked.
5. Fit. Connect that detail to your skills in one sentence.
6. Close. Say you look forward to the next step, and offer to send anything they need.
7. Sign-off. Your full name and phone number.

### Subject lines that work

- Thank you, [Job Title] interview
- Thank you for today's conversation
- Great to meet you, [Your Name]
- Following up on the [Job Title] interview
- Thank you, [Your Name] for [Job Title]

Keep the subject short and plain. The goal is that the interviewer opens it and knows what it is before they read it.

## 6 Thank You Email Templates

Replace every item in square brackets. Then read the email once out loud and cut anything that sounds stiff.

### 1. Standard thank you email

> Subject: Thank you, [Job Title] interview
>
> Hi [Interviewer Name],
>
> Thank you for meeting with me today about the [Job Title] role. I enjoyed hearing about [specific project or goal they mentioned].
>
> Our conversation made me even more interested in the role. My experience with [relevant skill or result] would help me contribute to [that project or goal] quickly.
>
> Please let me know if I can send anything else. I look forward to hearing about the next steps.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number]

### 2. After a phone or video screen

> Subject: Thank you for today's call
>
> Hi [Recruiter Name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to talk with me about the [Job Title] position at [Company]. It was helpful to learn more about [team, schedule, or process detail they shared].
>
> I am excited about the next step and happy to share any other information you need.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

### 3. After a panel interview (send one to each person)

> Subject: Thank you, [Job Title] panel interview
>
> Hi [Panel Member Name],
>
> Thank you for joining the panel today. I appreciated your question about [their specific question]. I have thought about it more since then, and [one extra sentence that adds to your answer].
>
> I enjoyed meeting the team, and I am excited about the chance to work on [team goal].
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

Change the specific question and the extra sentence for each person. Panel members sometimes compare notes, and identical emails look careless.

### 4. When you want to add something you forgot

> Subject: Thank you, and one more example
>
> Hi [Interviewer Name],
>
> Thank you for our conversation about the [Job Title] role today. When you asked about [topic], I did not mention [project or result]. At [Company or Team], I [one-sentence description of what you did and the result].
>
> I think this experience fits well with [the problem they described]. Thank you again for your time.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

### 5. Final round interview

> Subject: Thank you for today
>
> Hi [Interviewer Name],
>
> Thank you for meeting with me during the final round for the [Job Title] role. After talking with you and the team, I am even more sure that this is the role I want.
>
> I was especially glad to hear about [specific plan, value, or project]. I would bring [your top strength] to that work from day one.
>
> Thank you for the time you and the team gave to this process. I look forward to hearing from you.
>
> Sincerely,
> [Your Name]
> [Phone Number]

### 6. Short version (for a quick informal chat)

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks again for the chat today about [Job Title]. I really enjoyed hearing about [one detail]. I look forward to the next steps.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

## What Makes a Thank You Note Specific

The specific detail is the part that most notes miss. Without it, your email could go to anyone. Take notes right after the interview, while you remember the conversation. Write down:

- The name and title of each person
- One project or problem each person talked about
- One question that was hard for you, or interesting to them
- Anything they said about the next steps and the timeline

Then use one item from the list in each email. One real detail is better than three general compliments.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Sending the same text to every interviewer.
- Writing more than 200 words. Long notes are hard to read on a phone.
- Asking about salary or benefits. Save those questions for the recruiter or for a later stage.
- Sounding desperate. Do not write "I really need this job" or ask when they will decide.
- Spelling the interviewer's name wrong. Check it against their email signature or LinkedIn.
- Forgetting your contact details.
- Sending the note from an email address that is not professional. Use one with your name.

## What to Do After You Send It

If the interviewer replies, answer within one business day. If they do not reply, that is normal. Many interviewers do not answer thank you notes.

Sometimes the date they gave you passes with no decision. Wait two or three business days, then send a short follow-up email to the recruiter. Ask about the timeline and say that you are still interested.

Keep a record of who you thanked and when. A simple note in your calendar or a spreadsheet is enough.
